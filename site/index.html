Journalwright Studio

Design a journal.
Print it as a book.

A calm toolkit for making physical journals and planners that fit the way you live. Arrange the pages in a visual editor, get print-ready PDFs for Amazon KDP, and keep an e-ink reader in your pocket if you want one.

What you can do today

Everything here is built and on the main branch. The screenshots are from the editor demo with sample data only.

Block editor

Arrange a day page from blocks. See it at real size while you work, and get a warning before a page runs out of room.

  • 70+ palette items: check-ins, time lines, trackers, thought records, brain dump, sketch box and one-click presets.
  • Therapy, body and planning blocks, each with options and sizes.
  • A grid mode: blocks span several columns and rows, with overlap and minimum-size checks that explain themselves.

Try it: edit a day page

Book, spread and day

The editor opens on the whole book and zooms one step at a time. Pages open for viewing; the palette appears only when you press Edit.

  • Every page in spreads, with page ids, jump-to-page, a legend and a breadcrumb that always works.
  • View mode is clean and read-only. Edit and Done switch the tools on and off.
  • Switch between 5.5 x 8.5 and 8.5 x 11 and see the same pages at the new size.

Try it: open the book

Library and series

Every book gets a title of your own. Keep several books in one project, and group them in a series that shares defaults.

  • A shelf of covers: books, series and their order.
  • Series share a book length, cover and front matter; each book can still differ.
  • Zoom from library to series to book to spread to page.

Try it: browse the library

Unique pages and scan codes

Every page has its own id and scan code, and the build checks that none repeat, inside a book or across books. You decide how the scan marks look.

  • Send-to block, scanning border on or off, and the matrix code: position, size, format and an optional tiny label.
  • The header, the 9 pt frame and the code stay in fixed zones so every page can be identified.

Try it: open Scan settings

Print-ready output, scopes and volumes

KDP interiors and covers in 5.5 x 8.5 and 8.5 x 11, with an overflow check on every page and no Type 3 fonts.

  • A book can be a month, quarter, season, half-year, year, a custom range, or undated.
  • Past the paperback limit it splits itself into balanced volumes, each with its own cover, codes and manifest.
  • Profiles: one file says who and where: names, time zone, day start, trim and modules.

Versions, forks and merges

Optional accounts keep projects with full history. Guests keep local versions in the browser and never need an account to design or print.

  • History, compare with changed blocks marked, restore, branches and autosaved drafts.
  • Fork a shared project, propose changes back with page and block diffs, discuss them, then merge.
  • A three-way merge with a resolver that shows both versions when the same item changed on both sides.

Accessible by default

A full audit against WCAG 2.2, and a check in CI that fails on new problems.

  • Keyboard reordering of blocks, skip links, focus rings, 44 px targets and reduced motion.
  • Light and dark mode, and a layout that works on a 390 px phone.

E-ink companion

An optional Xteink X4 shows today without power and takes a one-press check-in. It stays offline.

  • Check-ins with zero-based and signed scales and pick-one items, and a silent focus timer.
  • The care split: what lives on paper and what lives on the device.
  • Settings for large text, bold type and a button remap; a hotspot page and Wi-Fi modes with a PIN.

See it in action

Four ways to use the same tools. Each opens the demo at the right place; the demo saves nothing.

A shift worker's 24 h time line

Nights one week, days the next: a page that shows the whole 24 hours.

  1. Open the editor and press Edit on any day.
  2. Add Work shift and Time line 24 h from the palette.
  3. Turn on Actual row and Shade night, so sunrise and sunset mark the dark hours.
  4. Drag the time line where you want it and press Done.

Build your own in three steps

  1. 1

    Make your profile

    Copy journal/content/profile.example.json and fill in your name, book title, place, time zone and the modules you want.

  2. 2

    Arrange the pages

    Open the editor, drag blocks onto the day page, check the Book view, and save your layout.

  3. 3

    Build and print

    One command writes every interior and cover PDF. Upload them to Amazon KDP as a private proof first.

cd journal && npm ci && npx playwright-core install chromium
KW_PROFILE=content/profile.example.json KW_OUT=out-sample node render.mjs month 2026-10 test.ics
KW_PROFILE=content/profile.example.json KW_OUT=out-sample node editor/build.mjs   # then open editor/dist/site/index.html

Privacy, as a promise

Journals are personal. The tools are built so that your data stays yours.

  • Calendars are never committed and never uploaded unencrypted.
  • Personal books leave the build only inside a password-protected archive.
  • This site, the editor demo and the sample cards use generic sample data only.
  • No account is needed to design and print. Accounts are optional, only for saving versions and sharing.
  • The e-ink companion sends nothing anywhere unless you start it. Today it only speaks over its own hotspot or a Wi-Fi network you set up on it. Syncing with your account, when it arrives, will only ever run when you press Sync, and health logs stay off unless you turn them on.

Roadmap

Only what is not finished yet. Everything above is ready to use today. Order can change, and nothing here is needed to design and print a book.

  • Page organiser

    In review

    Drag pages in the Book view to reorder, add, hide or remove them, with the codes and page ids kept straight.

  • Content packs and the pack ecosystem

    In progress

    Regional resource lists and transit as swappable data, plus packs made by artists and authors: themes, graphics, icon packs and declarative block packs. Every pack declares what it contains and is checked before use.

  • Puzzle pages and blocks

    Planned

    Crosswords and word searches as blocks and whole pages, and puzzle packs you can add to a book.

  • Block-based month, week and notes pages

    Planned

    The month, week and notes pages built from blocks on the grid, like the day page, so they can be rearranged too.

  • Spread days

    Planned

    A day can cover one page or a whole two-page spread, set per day, with per-weekday defaults. Both pages keep their own scan frame, code and header.

  • Releases and reusable pages

    Planned

    Freeze a checked version as a numbered print release with the exact PDFs and manifest kept. Reusable pages track their versions and where they are used.

  • Accessibility settings panel and print options

    Planned

    A settings panel for text size, contrast, reduced motion and dyslexia-friendly type. In print: large-print layouts, high-contrast ink, text-size scales, a dyslexia-friendly font and a left-handed layout.

  • Languages

    Planned

    The editor and this site in more languages, and printed books in them: message catalogs, fonts per script, right-to-left layouts and calendars.

  • Account profile and locations

    Planned

    Save places once; every new book uses them for sunrise, moon times, seasons, time zone and holidays. Your location never travels with a shared or forked project.

  • E-ink editor and sync

    Planned

    The companion can serve the editor on its own hotspot or your Wi-Fi, and sync with your account only when you start a sync on the device. Health logs stay off unless you turn them on.

  • More blocks and pages

    Planned

    Matrix and next-action blocks, commonplace and collection pages, a habit plan, a future log, a year-in-pixels page and more one-click layouts from the methods library.
