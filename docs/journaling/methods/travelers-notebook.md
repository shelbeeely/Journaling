# TRAVELER'S notebook (and "fauxdori" elastic covers)

A leather cover with an elastic cord along the spine that holds thin, swappable notebooks ("refills" or "inserts").
You build your own system by choosing which inserts to carry: a diary, a blank notebook, a pocket, a card file.
Made by TRAVELER'S COMPANY (formerly the MIDORI brand of Designphil), Japan, since 2006.

## 2. Summary

The TRAVELER'S notebook is a slim, roughly cut cowhide cover with a single elastic cord threaded through the spine,
a second elastic that wraps round the outside to close it, and a thin saddle-stitched notebook slipped under the spine
cord. Every other part is optional and swappable: more notebooks joined with connecting bands, pockets, a zip case, a
card file, dated diaries, brass clips, tin charms. Designphil's Atsuhiko Iijima made the prototype for a 2005 trade
show contest; it launched in 2006 with five refill types and grew into a brand (renamed TRAVELER'S COMPANY in 2015),
a Tokyo shop, and a worldwide community. Its promise is to treat every day like a journey: record what you meet, keep
tickets and scraps, and let the leather wear in. "Fauxdori" is the community name for elastic covers made by anyone
else, including DIY. For Keeping Watch, the most useful ideas are modular inserts (we already print a book per
month), a pocket for keeping paper scraps, a travel-style "places and things I met" log, and a passport-size carry
insert.

## 3. History and origin

### Who made it

Atsuhiko Iijima heads the TRAVELER'S division at Designphil, a Tokyo stationery design company (MIDORI was its
brand). Nippon.com (2017) reports he had earlier met a young couple running a small leather workshop in Chiang Mai
while on business in Thailand, and kept them in mind. When the International Stationery and Office Product Fair
Tokyo (ISOT) 2005 ran a contest for a slim, A5-sized notepad, Iijima rejected market-driven design, built a prototype
around simple leather and elastic, and took **second place**. Galen Leather describes a "team of three" at Designphil
that designed it and still works on it.

### Cultural context

- Launched into Japan's strong techo (planner) culture, where people buy a new diary each autumn and customise
  covers. See the techo notes in [jibun-techo.md](jibun-techo.md).
- It arrived as phones took over schedules. Iijima told Nippon.com that people "began to reevaluate the warmth of
  hand-held stationery items".
- The Chiang Mai leather is part of the story: natural marks and scratches are presented as character, not faults,
  and the cover darkens and softens with use.
- The fan culture (TRAVELER'S FACTORY visits, stamps you collect at the shop, caravans abroad, customisation photos on
  Instagram) made it a lifestyle brand, not just a product.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

1. **Every day is a journey.** You don't need a trip to keep a traveler's notebook; treat ordinary days with the
   attention you'd give a journey.
2. **Simple so you can make it yours.** TRC says the design was kept minimal "so people can customize it the way they
   want".
3. **Record what you meet.** Writing, sketching, and keeping tickets, maps and cards together.
4. **It ages with you.** Leather marks, stains and wear are part of your record.
5. **Free spirit.** The brand talks about a "Free Spirit" and the "constant action of seeking something".
6. **Modular, not all-or-nothing.** Finish one notebook, archive it, slip in the next. The cover lasts years.

**Problem it solves:** carrying several notebooks, or wanting one cover that changes with your life, without buying a
new planner each time your needs shift.

### 5.1 Cover
Regular cover holds refills about 110 × 210 mm (TRC lists refills as 8.2–8.3 × 4.3 in). The cover is a little bigger
than the refill. Passport cover holds 89 × 124 mm refills. Leather is thick, unlined, and supplied with a care balm
recommendation. Scratches and colour change are expected.

### 5.2 Elastic system
- **One insert:** open it to the centre spread and slide the spine elastic into the middle fold.
- **Two or three inserts:** hook a connecting band through the centre of a second insert and loop it round the first
  insert's cover, or round the spine elastic, so they stack like chapters.
- **Four or more:** community setups add elastics through extra spine holes (fauxdori covers often come with 2–5
  cords). Galen Leather says a standard cover takes about four inserts comfortably.
- **Closure:** the outer band holds the book shut and keeps loose papers in.

### 5.5 Pockets, folders, card files
Where tickets, receipts, photos and cards live. The kraft folder and zip case sit under their own elastic like a
refill.

### 5.6 Charms, clips, tags
Decoration and identity: a tin airplane charm tied to the spine cord, a brass clip as a page marker, a leather tag.
Purely optional.

### 5.7 Insert rotation and archiving
When a refill is full you take it out, date the cover, and store it (the 011 binder or a box). The cover goes on.

### 5.8 Ephemera and stamps
Pasting in tickets, maps and wrappers is central to the travel-journal side. TRAVELER'S FACTORY shops have stamps for
visitors to use.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (20–40 minutes)
1. Choose a size. Regular if you write a lot or want a weekly planner; Passport to carry in a pocket.
2. Decide what you need to carry every day. Most people start with two inserts:
   - a planner (monthly or weekly), and
   - a notebook (lined, grid or blank).
3. Add a pocket or kraft folder at the back for scraps.
4. Join inserts: the first under the spine cord; the second with a connecting band.
5. Write your name and a start date on each insert cover. Number them if you'll archive.
6. Optional: a pen holder and a charm.
7. Leave the leather alone. It will change on its own.

### The first day
- Open the planner insert; write today's plans.
- Open the notebook; write three lines about the day, or glue in a receipt, ticket or leaf.
- Close the band. Notice what you wished you had (more space? a pocket? grid?).

### The first week
- Days 1–3: use it as is.
- Day 4: move anything that bothers you (swap the order of inserts; add or remove one).
- Day 7: one page of "the week as a trip": places, people, one thing I met for the first time.

## 7. Daily practice

The cover has no daily method of its own; your inserts decide. A common TRC-style routine:

| When | Step | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Weekly or daily insert: write fixed plans | 1–2 min |
| During the day | Notebook: jot things you notice, sketch, collect a scrap into the pocket | — |
| Evening | Notebook: "today's journey" entry, paste in the day's scraps, add a stamp or small drawing | 5–10 min |
| Evening | Tick the planner | 30 sec |

**Prompts:** Where did I go today, even if nearby? What did I see for the first time? What did I keep (a ticket, a
leaf, a receipt)? Who did I meet? What would I tell someone who wasn't there?

## 8. Rhythms

- **Weekly:** plan the week in the weekly insert; clean out the pocket (paste or bin scraps); re-order inserts.
- **Monthly:** fill the monthly insert; look back at the month's pages; decide whether any insert is nearly full.
- **Quarterly / half-year:** dated weekly refills come as two six-month books; swap at July.
- **Yearly:** new diary set in autumn; archive the year's refills in the binder with a label; condition the leather if
  you like; replace tired elastics (repair kit).
- **Index upkeep:** optional. Some people keep a one-page index insert listing each archived refill by dates and topic.

## 12. Community practice

What people do (anecdotal; blogs, Instagram, YouTube):
- **Setups** posted as "what's in my TN": typically 2–4 inserts. Popular mixes: monthly + weekly + lined notebook +
  pocket; or bullet journal + sketchbook.
- **Hashtag scale:** Galen Leather counts about 3.6 million Instagram posts under #travelersnotebook (their figure,
  not checked).
- **Aesthetic camp:** stamps, washi, stickers, pasted ephemera, charms, patina photos, TRAVELER'S FACTORY stamp
  collecting.
- **Minimal camp:** one cover, one lightweight insert, a pen, nothing else.
- **Makers:** people print or sew their own inserts in any paper; many sell printable insert PDFs.
- **Leather care:** some condition the leather, most just use it. Water marks and scratches are shown off.
- **Events:** TRC "caravans" and shop events; users meet to swap stickers and show covers.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- **Pens:** MD paper takes fountain pens well; lightweight paper shows ghosting. Fine gel pens suit the passport size.
- **Paper weight:** standard MD refills are thin-to-medium; watercolour and kraft refills are heavier for mixed media.
- **Glue:** glue sticks or double-sided tape for ephemera; avoid wet glue on thin paper.
- **Stamps and stickers:** small rubber stamps (dates, icons), TRC customising stickers, washi tape.
- **Rulers:** TRC brass ruler, or any 15 cm ruler.
- **Elastics:** keep spares (repair kit) since they stretch.
- **Leather care:** optional balm.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

- **Apps:** GoodNotes and Notability TN-size templates (sold on Etsy); multi-notebook apps copy the "several inserts in
  one cover" idea with tabs.
- **E-ink:** reMarkable, Kindle Scribe and Boox can hold "inserts" as separate notebooks; no ephemera, no leather.
- **Printing your own inserts:** the most common hybrid. Print a PDF on A4 or letter, fold, stitch or staple, trim.
- **Scanning:** tall, narrow pages scan fine; pasted items make pages bulky and uneven.
- **Lost vs paper:** tactile ageing, ephemera, the feel of carrying one object.
- **Gained digitally:** unlimited inserts, search, no weight.

## 16. Evidence and research

No studies test the TRAVELER'S notebook or elastic-cover systems. What you write in it can draw on adjacent evidence:

| Study | Sample | Finding | Relevance and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harkin et al. (2016), *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis | 138 studies, N = 19,951 | Monitoring goal progress improves attainment (d+ = 0.40), more so when recorded physically | Applies to planner and tracker inserts; says nothing about covers or modularity |
| Kahneman et al. (2004), *Science*, Day Reconstruction Method | 909 employed women | Recalling the day as episodes gives a good record of time use and feelings | Supports a "today's journey" episode-by-episode entry; not an intervention |

**Claimed vs supported:**
- "Treating daily life like travel makes you notice more": claimed; related ideas (savouring, novelty) exist but
  aren't tested with this product.
- "Modularity keeps people journaling longer": claimed, untested.
- "Handmade leather that ages builds attachment": claimed.

## 17. Benefits

- Carry several notebooks as one (**claimed**, practical).
- Change your system without changing your book (**claimed**).
- Paper tracking inserts support goal progress (**evidenced**, adjacent: Harkin 2016).
- Episode-style daily entries give an accurate day record (**evidenced** as a method: Kahneman 2004).
- Ephemera make memories concrete (**claimed**).
- Durable cover, cheap refills over time (**claimed**; true once the cover is bought).
- Small, light, pocketable (Passport) (**claimed**, physical fact).

## 18. Pitfalls

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too many inserts; it bulges and won't close | Cap at three. Move reference inserts home |
| Buying inserts instead of writing | One rule: finish one before buying one |
| The planner insert is always in the wrong place | Put the insert you open most first (under the spine cord) |
| Pages can't be removed or rearranged | Use a separate insert per topic, or a fauxdori with more cords |
| Leather cost | Fauxdori or DIY; a cardboard or fabric cover works |
| Ephemera make it lumpy | Keep scraps in a folder; paste only the best on Sunday |
| Regular size is narrow for planning | Use Weekly + Memo, or a wider third-party size (B6, A5) |
| Elastic wears out | Repair kit or any round elastic cord |
| Losing older records | Label and archive each finished insert with dates |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD:** separate inserts reduce "where did I write that?"; keep only two inserts to limit choices. A pocket catches
  things you'd lose. The risk is buying inserts as a dopamine hit (see pitfalls).
- **Autism and sensory needs:** texture matters: leather smell, elastic snap, paper feel. A fabric fauxdori is quieter.
  A fixed insert order gives routine.
- **Chronic illness and low spoons:** Passport size is light to carry. Keep a symptom/meds insert that stays the same
  all year; everything else optional.
- **Depression and anxiety:** "one thing I met today" is a small, concrete prompt. Ephemera count as an entry on
  days with no words.
- **Trauma:** keep hard material in a separate insert you can remove and store away from the daily cover.
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia:** blank or dot inserts for drawing, stamps and pasted items; no ruling to follow.
- **Low vision:** Regular or larger third-party sizes, bold pens, high-contrast paper (MD cream may reduce glare for
  some; white for others).
- **Motor or hand pain:** elastics can be fiddly; use one insert, or a multi-cord cover so no connecting bands. Softer
  covers open flatter.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people:** a private insert for dysphoria, euphoria or HRT notes can be removed before a
  doctor's visit or a family visit. Names on insert covers can change as you do.
- **Shift workers:** the official weekly vertical only covers 8:00–22:00. Use the undated Weekly + Memo, a Jibun-style
  24-hour printed insert, or draw your own.
- **Low income:** fauxdori from cardboard, cloth or an old belt and a hair tie; inserts from folded printer paper.

## 24. FAQ

1. **Regular or Passport?** Regular for writing and planning; Passport for pocket carry and quick notes.
2. **How many inserts fit?** One comes standard; about three or four with connecting bands before it strains.
3. **Can I use other notebooks?** Yes, if they're thin and the right size. People make their own from folded paper.
4. **What's a fauxdori?** A non-TRC elastic notebook cover, often handmade.
5. **Is the leather supposed to scratch?** Yes. TRC presents wear as part of the notebook's character.
6. **Is there a dated planner?** Yes: dated monthly, weekly and weekly vertical sets each autumn, plus undated "free"
   diaries.
7. **Is the paper fountain-pen friendly?** MD paper refills handle fountain pens well; lightweight refills ghost.
8. **Does it lie flat?** Mostly, once the leather relaxes; thick stacks of inserts make it bow.
9. **Can I swap a single page?** No. Whole booklets swap, not pages. Use a separate insert per topic.
10. **Can it do a 24-hour day?** Not with official inserts (weekly vertical is 8:00–22:00). Print your own.

### e. Don't adopt

- **Real leather, charms, elastics** in the printed product: not possible in KDP, and ornament clashes with the calm,
  plain design.
- **Collecting inserts, stamps or charms as a goal**: invites buying and badges, which the project avoids.
- **Brand look or TRC names**: don't copy their trade dress or insert numbering.
- **8:00–22:00 timelines**: they drop night shifts; use full-day timelines instead.
- **Non-KDP trims inside the main book**: keep passport and TN-regular as optional home-print extras only.
