# Theme System Journal

A yearly theme instead of a resolution, a short daily page, and a dot tracker you can half-fill.

Research checked 2026-09-28. Points the sources couldn't settle are marked **(unverified)**.

---

## 2. Summary

The Theme System swaps a New Year's resolution for a **Yearly Theme**: a word or short phrase, like "Year of Reading" or "Year of Less", that names what you want more of and points in a direction instead of setting a target. Its central promise, in the official words, is that "a good theme can't fail." The Theme System Journal, made by Cortex Brand (Myke Hurley and CGP Grey), makes the idea physical. It has four theme pages where you write the theme, your "why" and the changes you hope to see. Then come 90 undated daily pages, each split into five lightly structured, **unlabelled** boxes you set up yourself; Grey's published pattern is where you are, one personal gratitude, one professional gratitude, a goal and free thoughts. Last come the **Daily Actions** tracker pages: 15 columns of circles you fill fully or halfway, so a partial effort still counts. One volume lasts about a season, so a theme gets a natural check-in every three months. For Journalwright Studio, the good parts are the no-fail framing, the half-fill dot, the short daily header and the seasonal cadence. All of them fit a calm, low-spoon practice.

### Cultural context

- **Resolution fatigue.** The idea caught on as a reaction to January resolutions that fail within weeks. The official site's example: someone resolves to "Read 50 books this year", ends up reading academic papers instead, and counts it as failure. Under a "Year of Reading", the same behaviour counts as success.
- **The productivity-podcast world of the 2010s.** Themes grew up next to OKRs, GTD and bullet journaling. They are the soft, directional member of that family.
- **The stationery community.** The journal was reviewed mainly by fountain-pen and notebook blogs, and it shows. The paper (100 gsm Munken Lynx), the lay-flat sewn binding and the tear-off corners are stationery-nerd features.

### Key quotes (official site)

> "A Yearly Theme is a word or phrase that represents what you'd like more of in your life, or the direction in which you'd like to go."

> "A good Theme is broad and acts as a guide, rather than a specific or numerical outcome."

> "Most importantly – a good theme can't fail."

> "The Theme System is flexible to accommodate your journey to a better life."

> Journal: "Empty your mind. Record your progress."

> Daily Actions: "Consider which actions, when completed consistently, will help you become the person you want to be."

## 4. Philosophy and principles

### Stated goals
- **More of something, not a finish line.** A theme names what you want more of ("It can be as small or as large as you like. Personal or professional.").
- **Direction over destination.** You judge a year by which way it trended, not by whether you hit a number.
- **No fail state.** Because a theme can't be failed, a bad week doesn't end the project. That is the main difference from a resolution.
- **Flexibility.** Four theme pages let you hold more than one theme, or change your mind partway through the year.
- **Connect to your "why".** The theme page asks what you hope to achieve and why it matters.

### Core beliefs
1. **Resolutions are brittle.** A specific target turns every deviation into failure, and failure makes people quit.
2. **Daily action is where change happens.** The theme sets the direction and the daily page records steps. The tracker shows the trend.
3. **Partial credit is real credit.** The half-filled circle exists to reward "some" rather than punish "not all".
4. **Structure should be light and yours.** The boxes are unlabelled on purpose: "So you can discover what works best for you."
5. **Seasons are a natural unit.** Grey talks about seasons and season breaks (#123), and one ~90-day journal is one season. A theme can be revised at each season boundary.

### The problem it solves
- The January start, February quit cycle.
- Goals that are too narrow to survive real life, like illness, a move or a new job.
- Journals that ask too much every day. A Theme System page can be done in minutes.
- Habit trackers that are all-or-nothing, where a missed or partial day looks like a blank.

## 5. Core components

| Element | What it is | Where it lives | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yearly Theme | A word or short phrase: "Year of ___" | Theme pages (4 in each journal) | Set yearly, revisited each season |

| Changes you're excited to see | A list of hoped-for changes | Theme page | Yearly or seasonal |
| Seasonal or quarterly theme | A narrower theme for one season, or a re-read of the yearly one | Theme page, or the first page of a new volume | Every ~3 months |
| Daily journal page | Five lightly structured, unlabelled sections | 90 journal pages | Daily |
| Daily header (Grey's pattern) | Where I am, personal gratitude, professional gratitude, today's goal | Top boxes of the daily page | Daily |
| Free space / thoughts | Open writing | The largest box | Daily |
| Daily Actions tracker | A grid of circles, 15 day-columns across, one row per action | 12 tracker pages | Daily |
| Half-fill | Fill half a circle for a partial effort | Tracker | Daily |
| Notes pages | Blank or ruled overflow | 13 notes pages | As needed |
| Index | A table of contents you fill in | 2 index pages | As needed |
| Tear-off corners | Perforated page corners you tear off to find today's page | Journal pages | Daily |

### 5.1 Yearly Theme
- A word or phrase: "Year of Reading", "Year of Less", "Year of Order", "Year of Foundations", "Year of Joy", "Year of People", "Year of Health", "Year of Me" and "Year of Creativity" all appear as examples on the official site.
- Myke's and Grey's own themes, from episode notes: "Year of Less" (Myke, 2017) and "Year of Redirection" (Grey, 2017).
- **Tests for a good theme:**
  - Can you think of ten different actions that would count? Then it's broad enough.
  - Can you tell whether a choice moves toward it or away from it? Then it's specific enough.
  - Would you still be glad of it if you only did 30% of what you imagined? Then it can't fail.

### 5.2 Theme page
The official site describes four prompts:
1. **Set your guide.** Write the theme.
2. **Connect to your "why".** "Describe what you're looking to achieve from your Theme and why it's important to you."
3. **Stay flexible.** Four pages allow several themes, or a change of mind.
4. **Focus on your goals.** "List the changes you're excited to see. This will help connect you to your big-picture goals."

### 5.3 Seasonal and quarterly themes
- Not a separate printed product. It's a practice that grew out of the podcast's talk of seasons and breaks (#123), and out of the journal lasting about one quarter.
- **Two common patterns:**
  - Keep the yearly theme and re-read it at the start of each volume, writing a one-line "this season, that means…".
  - Set a **season theme** of its own ("Season of Rest") inside or instead of the yearly one.
- How Grey defines and schedules his own seasons varies from year to year and isn't documented in writing **(unverified)**.

### 5.4 Daily journal page
- Officially "each page broken down into five sections for you to customise", with "multiple, unlabeled boxes."
- **What people put in them:**
  - Grey's pattern, as re-created by a reader: "Every day, I note where I am, one thing I'm personally and professionally grateful for, and one goal for the day", plus a "Today's thoughts" space (fixermark blog, 2024).
  - The Gentleman Stationer: gratitude, a quote from reading, significant events and theme connections, and current feeling in one word.
- **The five-section model this doc uses** (matching Grey's pattern):
  1. **Location**: where I am today.
  2. **Personal gratitude**: one thing.
  3. **Professional gratitude**: one thing (for someone who isn't working: "outside-of-home gratitude", or "work I did, paid or not").
  4. **Goal**: one goal for today.
  5. **Free space**: thoughts, events, whatever's on your mind.
- The exact printed box sizes and their order on the page aren't described in any source that could be opened. Treat the sketch in section 9 as an approximation **(unverified)**.

### 5.5 Daily Actions tracker (the dot tracker)
- Official copy: "A highly-personalized, visual habit tracker to log your progress… Habit tracking with a difference."
- Rows are actions you choose. Columns are days, **15 per page**. Fill a circle fully (done) or halfway (partly done); a reader on the official site calls "the half-split of the circles" "really useful".
- **12 tracker pages** cover the 90 days: six spreads of 15 days each would give exactly 90, which fits. The page-to-spread arrangement wasn't confirmed **(unverified)**.
- The number of rows (actions) per page is about 10 in common descriptions, but no official source states it **(unverified)**.
- **Three states:** empty (not done or not relevant), half (some), full (done). This is the same scale as Journalwright Studio's Habit dots block and the X4 companion's `dots` kind (0, 1, 2).

### 5.6 Notes and index
- 13 notes pages for overflow, lists or a season review.
- 2 index pages to note what's where (page numbers or dates).

### 5.7 Tear-off corners
- The daily pages have perforated corners. Tear off each finished day's corner, and the first intact corner marks today, so it works like a built-in bookmark.
- It also makes progress visible at the page edge.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (30–60 minutes, once a year or once a volume)
1. **Look back.** List three things from the last year you want more of and three you want less of.
2. **Draft five theme candidates.** Use "Year of ___". Write them fast; don't judge yet.
3. **Test each one** with the three tests in 5.1 (ten actions, a direction you can judge, glad at 30%).
4. **Pick one.** Two at most for a first try; Myke has run two (#79).
5. **Fill in the theme page:** the theme, your "why" (two or three sentences), and the changes you're excited to see (five to ten bullets).
6. **Pick 4–8 daily actions** for the tracker. Each one should be something that, if it happened most days, would look like your theme. Write them down the tracker's first column.
7. **Decide your five daily boxes.** Write the labels lightly in pencil on the first page, or on a sticky note you move forward each day.
8. **Set up the index.** Write "Theme – p.1", "Tracker – p.__" and leave the rest.

### First day
- Fill the five boxes. Keep it under five minutes; one line per box is fine.
- Fill today's tracker column, using half circles for partial efforts.
- Tear off the corner.

### First week
- Days 1–3: expect the box labels to feel wrong. Change them freely; that's the point of unlabelled boxes.
- Day 4 or 5: drop any tracker row you've left blank every day because it doesn't apply, and replace it.
- Day 7: read the week back (two minutes). Does the theme show up in the "goal" box at least a few times? If not, make the goal box theme-linked on purpose ("Theme step: ___").

## 7. Daily practice

**Order of operations, 3–10 minutes, morning or evening**

| Step | Time | What you do | Prompt |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 s | Date, and tear the corner off the last page | — |
| 2 | 20 s | **Location** | "Where am I?" (home, work site, bus, bed) |
| 3 | 40 s | **Personal gratitude** | "One thing I'm personally grateful for." |
| 4 | 40 s | **Professional or outside gratitude** | "One thing I'm grateful for at work, or out in the world." |
| 5 | 40 s | **Goal** | "One goal for today." In the evening: "Did it happen? What's tomorrow's?" |
| 6 | 1–6 min | **Free space** | "What's on my mind? What happened? Where did my theme show up?" |
| 7 | 20 s | **Tracker** | For each row: empty, half or full. |

**Morning or evening?**
- Grey's re-created pattern suits the morning (location, gratitude, goal to set the day), with free space written in the evening.
- Many users do the whole page at night as a review.
- Either works. What matters is keeping the time of day the same.

**Rules of thumb**
- One line per box is a complete entry.
- If you miss days, don't back-fill the journal pages. Do back-fill tracker circles if you remember honestly; leave them empty if you don't.
- A half circle is a win. Don't turn half-fills into a guilt metric.

## 8. Rhythms

| Rhythm | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Five boxes, tracker column, corner tear-off. | 3–10 min |
| **Weekly (optional)** | Look across the tracker spread. Count fulls and halves per row. Write one line in notes: "This week the theme looked like ___." | 5 min |
| **Every 15 days** | A tracker page fills up. Before turning it, keep, swap or drop each row. | 5 min |
| **Seasonal (~90 days, one volume)** | Re-read the theme page. Read ten random daily pages. Answer "Did the year trend toward the theme? What does it mean next season?" Start the new volume's theme page with the same theme, a refined one or a season theme. | 30–60 min |
| **Yearly** | Hurley and Grey's ritual (see the Yearly Themes episodes, December or January): review the old theme, set the new one. | 60–90 min |
| **Archiving** | Number the volumes on the spine: "2026-Q3 · Year of Rest". Keep the theme pages; they're the year's story. | — |
| **Index upkeep** | Add entries to the 2 index pages when you write something worth finding again. | seconds |

**Seasonal review questions**
1. What did my theme mean in practice this season?
2. Which tracker rows were mostly full? Mostly half? Mostly empty?
3. Which gratitude kept coming up?
4. What would "more of my theme" look like next season, in one sentence?
5. Keep, refine or replace the theme?

## 10. Worked examples

A generic sample person: shift worker, theme "Year of Rest", one season in.

### Sample theme page

```
THE YEAR OF  REST

WHY
I spent last year running on empty. I want rest to be something
I plan, not something that happens when I crash.

CHANGES I'M EXCITED TO SEE
- In bed by 11 on work nights, most nights
- One slow morning a week with no plans
- Saying no without writing a paragraph about it
- Fewer sick days from exhaustion
- Feeling like my days off are mine
```

### Sample day

```
DATE  Tue 14 Oct

(1) WHERE      Kitchen table, before shift
(2) PERSONAL   Warm socks out of the dryer
(3) WORK       Crew lead swapped my Saturday without fuss
(4) GOAL       Leave work on time; bed by 11
(5) FREE       Tired but not wrecked. Rest showed up as saying
               no to the extra hour. Want to try a 10-min lie-down
               after shift instead of scrolling. Rain all day.

Evening line: left on time ✓ · bed 11:20 (half)
```

### Sample 15-day tracker slice (days 1–7 shown)

```
DAILY ACTIONS        1  2  3  4  5  6  7
Bed by 11            ●  ◐  ●  ○  ●  ◐  ●
No phone in bed      ◐  ◐  ●  ○  ◐  ●  ●
Outside 10 min       ●  ●  ●  ●  ◐  ○  ●
Lie-down after shift ○  ◐  ●  ○  ●  ●  ◐
Water bottle         ●  ●  ●  ◐  ●  ●  ●
Stretch              ○  ○  ◐  ○  ◐  ●  ○
```

### Sample week, in notes

```
WEEK OF 13 OCT: REST
Fulls: 25  Halves: 11  Empty: 6
Rest looked like: leaving on time, the lie-down helps more than
scrolling. Stretch row isn't landing — try "stretch in shower"?
```

### Sample month / season summary

```
SEASON 4 (Oct–Dec) · YEAR OF REST
Trend: better. Bed-by-11 went from mostly half to mostly full.
Best gratitude repeats: warm things, quiet mornings, my crew.
Next season theme: keep REST; season focus "Rest in company"
(see friends without it costing a whole day off).
Drop: stretch row. Add: "one no without explaining".
```

## 11. Variations and offshoots

| Variant | Who | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Theme System (no journal)** | Hurley and Grey, on *Cortex* | Just the theme, kept in your head or a note app. The original form. |
| **Two themes** | Myke (#79) | One personal and one professional, or two areas. |
| **Seasonal themes** | Grew out of *Cortex*'s talk of seasons (#123) | A theme lasts one season, not a year. Good for people whose lives change by season (shift work, school terms, weather). |
| **Theme of the month** | Community | Twelve mini-themes. Easier to start, but loses the "long direction" benefit. |
| **Word of the Year / One Little Word** | Separate traditions (for example Ali Edwards' One Little Word) | Similar idea: one word as a lens. Usually more reflective, and not tied to daily tracking. |
| **Digital Theme System** | Readers (for example a 2024 Emacs Org-mode template) | Location, gratitude, goal and thoughts as a text snippet each day; the tracker as a table. |
| **Sidekick Notepad / Planner** | Cortex Brand | A separate, more structured daily planning pad (meetings, top priorities, tasks). Not a theme tool, but made to pair with the journal. The product name "Sidekick Planner" appears in search listings; the official site lists "Sidekick Notepad" **(unverified whether both exist)**. |
| **"Theme System Planner" / "Theme System Notebook"** | — | No current Cortex Brand product by either name was found. "Theme System Notebook" seems to be a reviewer's name for the journal **(unverified)**. |

## 12. Community practice

- **Where people talk:**
  - the r/CGPGrey subreddit, linked from the #150 notes;
  - the Cortex YouTube channel and Relay FM members' areas;
  - stationery blogs (The Pen Addict, The Gentleman Stationer);
  - Instagram and Threads @cortexbrand.
- **What people actually do:**
  - **Label the boxes once and keep them.** Gratitude, a quote, events and a feeling word (Gentleman Stationer), or location, two gratitudes and a goal (Grey's pattern).
  - **Use it loosely.** One official testimonial: "Even when I slack off and don't use it for weeks, I can come back and pick up where i left off." Another: "I am not journaling everyday but … a gentle push as far as structure."
  - **Track theme-adjacent rows,** not every habit: "a longer workout, or spend more time reading."
  - **Use it for work too.** "Both for tracking work productivity and personal growth journaling."
  - **Put it in a cover.** The size fits many A5 covers (Roterfaden Taschenbegleiter, Lochby Field Journal).
- **Common customisations:**
  - a different second-colour pen for half-fills;
  - a monthly one-line summary in the notes pages;
  - stickers or washi to mark season boundaries;
  - a "theme connection" line added to the free space.
- **Aesthetic vs minimalist:**
  - The journal pulls minimalist. Grey, dark-grey covers, and no printed prompts or decoration.
  - Decorative users add stickers, but the product doesn't push it.
- **Critiques:**
  - Price across a year ("I like it, but I don't $80 like it" — Pen Addict review).
  - Some don't know what to put in the unlabelled boxes ("I still haven't figured out how I'll use the boxes").

  - Paper keeps the tactile "tear the corner" ritual and the calm of no screen.

## 16. Evidence and research

No study has tested the Theme System or its journal directly. Its parts line up with better-studied ideas.

| Study | Sample and design | Finding | Relevance | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oscarsson, Carlbring, Andersson & Rozental (2020)**, *PLOS ONE* | 1,066 adults making New Year's resolutions; randomised to no, some or extended support; followed to 12 months | At one year, **approach-oriented goals beat avoidance goals (58.9% vs 47.1%)**. The group with *some* support did best (62.3%); extended, more detailed support did worse (52.5%). The authors suggest over-specified goals create more chances to feel you've failed. | Supports phrasing themes as "more of X" (approach), and hints that less rigid structure can help. | Self-reported success; a Swedish online sample; resolutions, not themes. |
| **Emmons & McCullough (2003)**, *J Pers Soc Psychol* | Three studies: students (weekly or daily logs) and adults with neuromuscular disease | Gratitude listing raised well-being on several, not all, measures; the effect on positive affect was most robust. | Supports the two daily gratitude boxes. | Small to moderate effects; mostly students; later replications are mixed. |
| **Harkin et al. (2016)**, *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis | 138 studies, N = 19,951 | Monitoring interventions raised goal attainment (d+ = 0.40); effects were larger when progress was reported or made public, or physically recorded. | Supports the Daily Actions tracker. | Very mixed study types; effect depends on how monitoring is done. |
| **Locke & Latham (2002)**, *American Psychologist* | Review of 35 years of goal-setting research | Specific, hard goals usually beat "do your best" for performance. | **A counterpoint.** For measurable tasks, vague themes may do worse than specific goals. The Theme System pairs a vague theme with a specific daily goal box. | Mostly workplace task performance, not life direction or wellbeing. |
| **Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)**, *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology*, meta-analysis | Implementation-intention studies | If-then plans reliably help people act on goals. | Suggests turning the "goal" box into "If ___, then I'll ___" for harder days. | Lab and field mix. |
| **Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts & Wardle (2010)**, *Eur J Soc Psychol* | 96 people, 12 weeks, one new daily behaviour each | Time to 95% automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days. **Missing one opportunity did not materially affect habit formation.** | Supports tracker rows kept for at least a season, and the no-fail stance on missed days. | Small sample; self-report automaticity. |
| **Pennebaker (1997)**, *Psychological Science* | Review | Writing about emotional experiences is linked to health benefits. | Weak support for the free-space box (daily notes are shorter and less emotional than the study tasks). | Effects vary; the studied tasks are 15–20 min of focused writing. |

**Claimed vs supported**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| "Themes succeed where resolutions fail" | Plausible, supported indirectly by the approach-goal finding. Not tested directly. |
| "A good theme can't fail" | A framing choice, not an empirical claim. Its value is psychological: less fear of failing. |
| Gratitude improves wellbeing | Some studies, with modest effects. |
| Tracking helps you act | Supported by meta-analysis. |
| Half-fills keep motivation up | Not studied. Plausible, given the finding that a missed day doesn't reset a habit. |

## 18. Pitfalls

| Problem | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The theme is too vague ("Year of Better") | Broad slides into meaningless | Use the ten-actions test. If you can't name ten, narrow it ("Year of Better Sleep"). |
| The theme is secretly a resolution ("Year of 50 Books") | Old habits | Take the number out. Keep the number as one tracker row if you want it. |
| Blank boxes cause paralysis | The unlabelled design | Use Grey's pattern for the first 15 days, then adjust. |
| The tracker becomes a guilt wall | Too many rows, rows that don't fit | At most 6 rows. Drop any row that's empty for 5 days in a row. |
| Half-fills feel like failure | Perfectionism | Write "◐ = success" at the top of the tracker. Count halves and fulls together as "showed up". |
| Losing momentum between volumes | The journal ends every 90 days | Buy or set up the next one before day 80, and put the season review on the calendar. |
| Cost across a year | $80–100 for four volumes | Any notebook works: draw the boxes, or print a template. |
| Professional gratitude doesn't fit | Unemployed, retired, disabled, carer | Rename it "outside me": the world, a stranger, a service, a place. |
| Forgetting the theme exists by March | It isn't on the daily page | Write the theme word at the top of each tracker page, or in the goal box. |
| Doing it only when things are good | Journal avoidance on bad days | A minimum entry: location plus one tracker column is a full day. |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD.**
  - Short, fixed boxes help. Keep one pen clipped to the journal and do the page at the same trigger (after coffee, after clocking out).
  - Use tear-off corners as the "where am I" marker.
  - Theme per season, not per year, for novelty.
  - Allow gaps: the official testimonial about picking up after weeks away is the model.
- **Autism and sensory needs.**
  - Fixed labels and the same order every day.
  - Plain, calm pages; the official journal has no decoration.
  - Replace gratitude with "one thing that felt OK" if gratitude language feels forced.
  - Add a sensory row to the tracker.
- **Chronic illness and low spoons.**
  - Define a "minimum day" (location plus tracker).
  - Half-fill means "did a smaller version".
  - Pick rows that are care, not productivity (meds, water, rest).
  - Themes like "Year of Pacing" work well.
- **Depression and anxiety.**
  - The no-fail frame matters most here.
  - Avoid rows you're likely to leave empty every day.
  - Gratitude can be tiny ("the bus came").
  - If free space turns into rumination, cap it at three lines and end with the goal.
- **Trauma.**
  - Keep the page present-focused (location, today). No prompt should require revisiting the past.
  - Themes should be about safety or agency ("Year of Choosing").
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia.**
  - Use icons or single words in the boxes.
  - The tracker is almost writing-free, and dots beat words.
  - Voice memo for the free space, with the date written.
- **Low vision.**
  - Use the 8.5×11 size or print bigger circles (8 mm or more).
  - A thick marker for fills, high-contrast black on white, a large-print theme word.
- **Motor or hand pain.**
  - Dots and half-dots need less writing.
  - Use a fat-barrel pen.
  - Tick instead of fill (the official site allows using circles as checkboxes).
  - Dictate the free space.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people.**
  - Themes can hold transition goals without deadlines ("Year of Becoming", "Year of Voice").
  - Tracker rows for HRT doses, voice practice or body care.
  - Location and gratitude lines hold small euphoria moments.
- **Shift workers.**
  - "Day" means from waking to sleeping, not a calendar date.
  - Write the date plus "shift / off".
  - Location is especially useful (site, yard, home).
  - Seasonal themes can follow shift-bid periods.
- **Low income.**
  - The method is free: any notebook, or a printed page.
  - Five boxes and a 15-column grid fit on one sheet of copy paper.
  - Skip the subscription.

## 24. FAQ

1. **Do I need the official journal?**
   No. The system is a theme plus daily reflection plus a tracker, and any notebook works. The journal is a nice, ready-made version.
2. **How is a theme different from a goal?**
   A goal has a finish line; a theme has a direction. You can still set goals inside a theme, in the daily goal box for example.
3. **Can I have more than one theme?**
   Yes. The journal has four theme pages for this reason, and Myke ran two themes in 2019. Two is a sensible maximum.
4. **What if I pick the "wrong" theme?**
   Change it. The official site says the four pages are there so you can change your mind. A season boundary is a natural moment.
5. **What goes in the unlabelled boxes?**
   Anything. Grey's pattern: where you are, personal gratitude, professional gratitude, and today's goal, plus thoughts. Try it for 15 days, then adjust.
6. **What does a half-filled circle mean?**
   You did some of it, or a smaller version. It counts.
7. **I missed two weeks. Now what?**
   Start at today's page. Don't back-fill. Official testimonials praise exactly this: "I can come back and pick up where I left off."
8. **Should the theme start on 1 January?**
   It doesn't have to. Hurley and Grey often record their themes episode in December or January, but any season boundary works.
9. **Is there evidence it works?**
   Not for the system itself. Its parts (approach goals, gratitude, progress monitoring) have some support; see section 16.
10. **Morning or evening?**
    Either. The morning suits location, gratitude and goal; the evening suits free space and the tracker. Many people split it.
11. **How many tracker rows?**
    Start with 4–6. More rows make it more likely to become a guilt wall.
12. **Is there a digital version?**
    No official one was found. A PDF template on a tablet or e-ink device works; see section 15.

### e. Don't adopt

- **Tear-off corners.** Not possible on KDP, and they would cut into the scan frame.
- **Unlabelled boxes by default.** Blank boxes are paralysing for many ND users. Ship labelled, and let people rename.
- **"Professional gratitude" wording.** Shift workers, students, carers and people between jobs all use this system, and not everyone has a job. Use "World" or "Out there".
- **Yearly-only themes.** Offer seasonal ones. They fit shift bids and places with strong seasons.
- **Subscription, or any "streak".** No counters or "days in a row" on print or X4. It works against the no-fail idea and the firmware scope.
- **Premium paper claims.** KDP paper isn't Munken Lynx. Don't imply it.
