# The Bullet Journal Method (Ryder Carroll)

A reference for the original Bullet Journal ("BuJo"): what it is, where it came from, how every part works, how people bend it, and what Keeping Watch should take from it.

Checked against sources on 2026-09-28. Where a detail couldn't be confirmed from a primary source, it's marked **(unconfirmed)**.

## 2. Summary

The Bullet Journal is an analog system for tracking tasks, events and notes in one notebook.

- **Rapid logging.** You write short, symbol-tagged lines: `•` task, `○` event, `–` note.
- **Four kinds of page.** Entries sit in a few "collections": an **Index**, a **Future Log** (the months ahead), a **Monthly Log** (a calendar page plus a task page) and a **Daily Log** you write as the day happens. Any topic can also get its own custom collection.
- **Migration.** At the end of each month you rewrite unfinished tasks forward (`>`), schedule them into the future (`<`), or strike them out. The effort of rewriting is a filter: "If an entry isn't worth the effort to rewrite, then it's probably not that important."
- **Why it exists.** Ryder Carroll built it over years to cope with his own ADHD. He now presents it as "a mindfulness practice that works like a productivity system".
- **Where it went.** It spread through the internet from 2013. It gained a book (2018), official Leuchtturm1917 notebooks and a companion app. It also grew a huge artistic "bujo spread" culture, which is often at odds with the plain original.

## 3. History and origin

### Who created it
- **Ryder Carroll.** He was diagnosed with attention deficit disorder as a child. He began piecing together a paper method in college in the late 1990s to "move past his learning disabilities" (Wikipedia, summarising press interviews).
- **Career.** By his own bio, he became "the youngest art director at a major US fashion brand". He started companies and was creative head of a New York City design agency. Wikipedia notes he also worked as "an app, web, and game designer" and had an interest in scrapbooking.
- **His own account.** In "Inside ADHD" (bulletjournal.com, 14 October 2019) he describes ADHD as "trying to catch the rain", and writes: "The Bullet Journal Method is the result of me trying to get better at catching the rain."
- **In his TEDxYale talk:** "A big misconception about ADD is that we can't focus. I could focus. I was just focusing on too many things."

### Cultural context
- BuJo arrived in 2013, when productivity culture was mostly apps (Evernote, Wunderlist) and GTD was widely followed. Tech blogs such as Lifehack.org and Lifehacker spread it as an analog alternative.
- It then met Instagram and Pinterest. The artistic "spread" culture drove a stationery boom (Wikipedia notes rising sales of notebooks and pens). It also created the belief that a bullet journal must be beautiful, which Carroll has pushed back on. The official site now calls itself a place for "intentional living".

### Key quotes from the creator
- "Holding thoughts in your mind is like trying to grasp water — it's nearly impossible." (TEDxYale)
- "Being busy doesn't mean that you're being productive. Being busy just means you're in a state of being functionally-overwhelmed." (TEDxYale)
- "If an entry isn't worth the effort to rewrite, then it's probably not that important. Get rid of it." (bulletjournal.com, Migration)
- "True productivity starts by becoming aware of Why we do what we do." (bulletjournal.com, Ryder Carroll page)
- "Figure out ways to be less busy so that you can be more productive." (Yearly Migration, 2015)

## 4. Philosophy and principles

**Stated goal.** "Track the past, order the present, design the future." The site's definition is "a mindfulness practice that works like a productivity system", with two halves:
- **The System:** the notation and the pages, for organising thoughts.
- **The Practice:** reflection and intention, for checking that what you do matches what you value.

**Core beliefs**
1. **Externalise to declutter.** Thoughts held in the head are slippery. Written down, they can be sorted.
2. **Busy is not productive.** The aim is to do fewer, more meaningful things.
3. **Friction is a feature.** Hand-rewriting a task costs effort, so migration forces the question "is this worth it?"
4. **Build as you go.** You don't pre-print a year. Set up each month at the end of the previous one: "You never know how many pages you may use in any given month."
5. **Form follows need.** Every page is optional except the ones that help you. Collections are invented on demand.
6. **Intention over aesthetics.** The book and site put "Why?" above looks.

**The problem it solves.**
- For Carroll, the problem was scattered attention: sticky notes everywhere, several apps, nothing trusted.
- BuJo replaces that with **one inbox, one notation and one review loop**, cheap enough to keep up on a bad day.

**The mental inventory.** This is a set-up exercise from the TEDx talk and the book. Write three lists: things you **need** to do, **should** be doing and **want** to do. For each item ask whether it's vital, and whether it matters to you or someone you love. "If your answer is no to both of those, you've just identified a distraction, and you can cross it off your list."

## 5. Core components

| Element | Kind | Symbol or place | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid logging | Notation | Short lines plus a bullet | Capture fast, sort at a glance |
| Task | Bullet | `•` | Something to do |
| Task done | Bullet state | `X` (drawn over the dot) | Completed |
| Task migrated | Bullet state | `>` | Moved forward to a later log or a collection |
| Task scheduled | Bullet state | `<` | Moved back into the Future Log (a specific later month) |
| Irrelevant task | State | Line struck through the whole entry | Dropped on purpose |
| Event | Bullet | `○` | Dated happening, before or after it occurs |
| Note | Bullet | `–` | Fact, idea, thought or observation |
| Priority | Signifier | `*` left of the bullet | Matters most |
| Inspiration | Signifier | `!` | A great idea, usually on a note |
| Explore | Signifier | Eye symbol | Needs research or follow-up |
| Nesting | Layout | Indent under a parent | Sub-tasks or context |
| Topic and page number | Layout | Heading on each page, number at the foot | Makes the page indexable |
| Index | Collection | First pages | Table of contents built as you go |
| Future Log | Collection | After the index | Months ahead |
| Monthly Log | Collection | Two facing pages each month | Calendar page and task page |
| Weekly Log | Collection (later, optional) | A spread per week | Week overview |
| Daily Log | Collection | Written each day | The running inbox |
| Custom collection | Collection | Anywhere | A topic page: a list, a project or a tracker |
| Threading | Navigation | Previous and next page numbers by the folio | Links split collections |
| Migration | Ritual | Monthly and yearly | Filter and carry forward |
| Reflection | Ritual | Daily (morning and night) and at migration | Check intention |
| Key | Reference page | Optional, often inside the cover | Reminds you of the symbols |

### 5.1 Rapid logging
- **The language of BuJo.** You write "Bought cake and went home", not "I bought cake at the local bakery today and went home".
- **Use your own words.** Keep entries short but clear enough to make sense in a month.
- **Nest by indenting.** Put sub-tasks or context under their parent.

### 5.2 Bullets
- **Task `•`**: "actionable items like 'Pick up dry cleaning'".
- **Event `○`**: "date-related entries that can either be scheduled … or logged after they occur". Carroll advises keeping events objective and brief, with feelings as nested notes if wanted. That's how the book and videos describe it, but the exact wording is **(unconfirmed)**.
- **Note `–`**: "facts, ideas, thoughts, and observations". Notes aren't immediately actionable.
- **Task states**
  - `X` means complete.
  - `>` means migrated forward.
  - `<` means scheduled: sent back to the Future Log for a later month.
  - A strike-through, drawn "starting from the center of Task Bullet itself", marks a task as no longer relevant.

### 5.3 Signifiers
- They go to the **left** of a bullet and add context.
  - `*` priority.
  - `!` inspiration.
  - An eye means explore: "something that requires further research, information, or discovery".
- **Custom signifiers are allowed.** Carroll's line is to keep them few.
- **Edition 2's key page** uses dot boxes, so people can add colour codes.

### 5.4 Index
- **Set-up.** "Simply leave the first couple pages of your notebook blank." As you add collections, write the topic and its pages, for example "New Zealand Trip: 5–10, 23, 34–39".
- **What to index.** Log the Monthly Logs, which gives a timeline of the notebook. Don't index each Daily Log.
- **Sub-indexes.** Separate indexes per project or class are a documented variant.
- The official notebook pre-prints index pages.

### 5.5 Future Log
- "An overview of your year." Set it up by "graphing the pages by the amount of months you'll need."
- **Standard layout.** Two equally spaced horizontal lines across a spread make six month boxes (three per page).
- **What goes in.** Tasks and events for months beyond the current one.
- **At each new month,** check the Future Log and migrate that month's items into the new Monthly Log.
- **Alternatives** include the Alastair Method, the Calendex and year-at-a-glance calendars. See [alastair-method.md](alastair-method.md).

### 5.6 Monthly Log
- **Calendar page (left).** "List all the dates of that month down the left margin, followed by the first letter of the corresponding day", e.g. `14M`. Log events briefly, either ahead of time or afterwards as a record.
- **Task page (right).** New tasks for the month, plus "unfinished Tasks that you've Migrated from the previous month or the Future Log".
- **Timing.** "Set up the next Monthly Log at the end of the current month, and not way ahead of time."

### 5.7 Daily Log
- **The heading** is today's date, as the topic.
- **Through the day,** "Rapid Log your Tasks, Events, and Notes as they occur."
- **Paging.** Don't give each day its own page in advance. If a page isn't full, the next date starts where you left off. This keeps it cheap and stops blank pages from shaming you.
- **End of day.** Review, mark done, and migrate or drop.

### 5.8 Weekly Log (later addition, optional)
- **Not in the 2013 core.** The 2023 FAQ adds it: "an overview of your week."
- **Official suggestion.** Write the week's dates down the left page for time-specific events. Put the tasks and notes for the week on the right page, and migrate unfinished tasks to the next week.
- It's also the most copied community spread. Many people run weekly-only without Daily Logs.

### 5.9 Collections
- **Definition.** Any themed page: shopping or reading lists, classes, projects.
- **How to add one.** Make a spread with a topic, move related entries there, then add it to the index. If it runs out of room, continue elsewhere under the same topic and **thread** it.
- **Timing.** "A great time to create and update your collections is during the monthly migration."
- The four core logs are themselves called collections.

### 5.10 Threading
- Carroll, 18 April 2015: "simply add the page number of the previous or next instance of that collection next to the current page number."
- **Example.** Pages 2–6 → 14–21 → 45–62. You follow the chain without going back to the index.
- **Across notebooks.** "Yearly Migration" (2015) suggests the form `7.125`, meaning notebook 7, page 125.

### 5.11 Migration
- **Monthly.**
  - Go through the month. Mark finished tasks `X`.
  - Strike out tasks that no longer matter.
  - Turn a live task's `•` into `>` and rewrite it on the new Monthly Log's task page or in a collection.
  - Use `<` for tasks that belong to a later month's Future Log.
- **Purpose.** "to distill the things that are truly worth the effort … and to separate the signal from the noise."
- **Yearly, or when a notebook fills.** The steps below are from Carroll (2015):
  1. Review and reflect.
  2. Close loops.
  3. Prepare and break in the new notebook.
  4. Migrate only the collections that "still matter".
  5. Set up January.
  6. Write the start and end dates on the spine or edge of the old notebook, then archive it.

### 5.12 Reflection
- **The Practice half.** The site asks whether each task is important, necessary and life-improving.
- **Timing.** The book describes a daily morning reflection (plan) and evening reflection (review and let go), with deeper reflection at migration. Our summary; exact book wording **(unconfirmed)**.
- The Companion app adds monthly reflection prompts.

### 5.13 Book-era practice concepts
The 2018 book adds a "Practice" section. It covers intention, reflection, meaning, goals, small steps ("sprints"), time, gratitude, control and imperfection, plus exercises such as the **mental inventory** and a goal-listing exercise commonly called **5, 4, 3, 2, 1**: goals for the next 5 years, 4 months, 3 weeks, 2 days and 1 hour.

**Unconfirmed:** the chapter list above is from secondary summaries and memory. Verify it against the text before quoting.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (30–60 minutes)
1. **Choose any notebook.** Number the pages if they aren't numbered: odd pages bottom-right, even bottom-left.
2. **Optional key.** Inside the front cover, write `• task · X done · > migrated · < scheduled · ○ event · – note · * priority · ! idea · eye explore`.
3. **Index.** Title pages 1–2 (or 1–4) "Index" and leave them blank.
4. **Future Log.** On the next spread, draw two horizontal lines per page to make six boxes. Label the next six months after this one. Fill in known birthdays, trips and deadlines.
5. **Monthly Log.** On the next spread:
   - Left page: write the dates 1–31 with the day letter, then log known events.
   - Right page: tasks for this month.
6. **Mental inventory (optional but recommended).** On a spare page, write need / should / want lists. Cross out what fails "vital or meaningful?", then move survivors to the monthly task page or the Future Log.
7. **Index it.** Add "Future Log: 3–4" and "October: 5–6".
8. **Daily Log.** Start on the next page with today's date.

### First day
- **Morning (2 minutes).** Write the date, copy today's events from the monthly calendar, and add 3–5 tasks.
- **During the day.** Log in 5–10 word lines as things come up: tasks, events after they happen, notes.
- **Evening (3 minutes).** `X` what's done. Leave the rest open. Add one note about how the day went, if you like.

### First week
- **Days 2–7.** Start each new date right under yesterday's entries. Carry forward the tasks you still want, marking the old ones `>`.
- **Collections.** When a topic keeps appearing (say, "Apartment move"), give it a collection spread, index it and move its tasks there.
- **End of week (optional).** Flip back, notice what keeps getting migrated, and decide whether to do it, schedule it (`<`) or drop it.
- **Don't** set up decorative trackers yet. Carroll's advice is to learn the core first, then personalise.

## 7. Daily practice

| When | Time | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning plan** | 2–5 min | 1. Write the date as the topic. 2. Check the Monthly Log calendar and copy today's events. 3. Check yesterday's open `•` and migrate the ones you'll do (`>`), dropping the rest. 4. Mark 1–3 with `*`. 5. Optional reflection: "What matters today?" |
| **Through the day** | Seconds each | Rapid log as it happens. Tasks go in the moment they appear, so the mind can let them go. Use `!` for ideas. Log events after, with nested notes for detail |
| **Evening review** | 3–5 min | 1. `X` the done ones. 2. For each open task ask "still worth it?", then migrate, schedule or strike it. 3. Add notes: what happened, one feeling, one good thing. 4. Optional prompt: "What can I let go of?" |

**Order of operations:** date → events → migrated tasks → new tasks → priorities → log all day → review → migrate.

**Prompts** used by the official material and the community:
- What's on my mind?
- What's vital, and what's just loud?
- Why am I doing this?
- What did I learn today?
- What am I grateful for?

## 8. Rhythms

| Rhythm | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | Plan, log, review | 5 min |
| **Weekly** (optional) | Set up the weekly log. Migrate the week's leftovers. Look at the Future Log for the next two weeks | 10–15 min |
| **Monthly** | New Monthly Log. Migrate from the old month and the Future Log. Update collections. Add to the index. Reflect on what kept moving | 20–45 min |
| **Quarterly** | No official ritual. Community practice is to extend the Future Log another three months and review goal collections. Also a natural point for a "5, 4, 3, 2, 1" refresh | 15–30 min |
| **Yearly or new notebook** | Yearly Migration: review the whole notebook, close loops, set up the new index and Future Log, migrate only living collections, label the spine with dates, archive | 1–2 h |
| **Index upkeep** | Add each new collection and Monthly Log as you create it, not later | Seconds |
| **Archiving** | Shelf of numbered notebooks. The `7.125` references let new notebooks point at old pages | — |

## 10. Worked examples

### A day (Tuesday 7 Oct, shift day)
```
10.07.Tu
X Meds AM
○ Shift 6:00-14:00
  - 38°F at start, wind; gloves helped
X Buy bus pass
* • Email clinic about refill        <- priority
> • Call landlord re: heater         <- migrated to 10.08
• Library holds                      <- struck: not needed now (drawn through)
○ Walked home the long way
  - saw the first hard frost
! - Put bus times on phone lock screen?
- Energy low after 3pm; ate late
eye - Is there a winter bus schedule change?
```
Evening review:
- The clinic email is done → `X`.
- "Call landlord" → `>` and rewritten under 10.08.
- "Library holds" → struck out.

## 11. Variations and offshoots

| Variant | Creator | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Alastair Method** | Alastair Johnston, 2015 | Column-dot future log. Later a context to-do (2020) and a Kanban project board (2023). See [alastair-method.md](alastair-method.md) |
| **Calendex** | Eddy Hope (idea from 2013–14, featured 10 Jan 2016) | A calendar-index grid of months × days 1–31. Each cell holds the **page number** where an event's details live |
| **Hybrid future log** | Veronika (@onki_art) | Month mini-calendars on top, a table of date / event / time / done below |
| **Sticky-note future log** | Community | Future items on sticky notes that move forward page to page |
| **Weekly-first BuJo** | Community, later official | A weekly log replaces most daily pages |
| **Dutch door** | Community | Cut or short pages so a weekly or monthly calendar shows beside daily pages. Creator **unconfirmed** |
| **Minimalist / "basic" BuJo** | Community (e.g. r/basicbulletjournals) | The core only: black pen, no art |
| **Artistic / "bujo spreads"** | Community (Instagram, YouTube) | Themed monthly covers, hand lettering, trackers, washi |
| **Digital BuJo** | Various apps | Rapid logging in software (see §15) |
| **Rolling calendar or rolling week** | Community | A continuous date list that migrates without monthly resets |

## 12. Community practice

- **Where.** Reddit (r/bulletjournal for art and core; r/basicbulletjournals for minimalist), Instagram (#bujo), YouTube, Pinterest, and the official site's "Bulletjournalist" blog.
- **What people actually do**
  - **Weekly spreads over daily logs.** This is common because a spread shows the week.
  - **Trackers.** Habit grids, mood "year in pixels", sleep, water, period and spending.
  - **Collections.** Books read, gratitude, brain dump, goals, packing and gift lists.
  - **Colour coding.** This is why Edition 2's key uses dot boxes.
  - **Monthly cover pages.** A theme and quote, then the log.
  - **Future-log swaps.** Calendex or Alastair columns replace the six-box log.
- **Aesthetic versus minimalist.** The artistic camp treats the journal as a creative hobby and a calm ritual. Critics, and many ADHD users, say the art turned a 5-minute tool into an hour-a-week project and a source of comparison. Carroll's site keeps pointing back to intention and the simple core. Many people alternate between the two by season.
- **Common customisations**
  - Custom signifiers: `$` money, `@` person, `?` question, `♡` good thing.
  - Nested time stamps.
  - Numbered priorities.
  - A "brain dump" page before the monthly log.
  - A "quote of the month".
  - A "Done" list for low-energy days.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- **Notebook.** Any with numbered pages. Dot grid is the default because it works for both lists and lines. 80 gsm ghosts; 100–120 gsm handles fineliners and some fountain pens.
- **Pens.**
  - One black pen is enough.
  - Fineliners (0.3–0.5 mm) are popular because they don't bleed.
  - Gel pens smear on coated paper.
  - Fountain pens need 100 gsm or more.
- **Ruler.** For Future Log lines. The pocket guide works as one.
- **Optional.** Pencil for planning layouts, a highlighter for priorities (one colour), date stickers, washi tape for tabs, sticky notes for moving items.
- **Stamps and stencils.** Popular for recurring layouts in the artistic camp. Not needed for the method.
- **Bleed test.** Try each pen on the last page first.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

- **Official.** The Companion app is a capture buffer. Entries expire after 72 hours, which pushes you to migrate them to paper. Reviewers like that.
- **General apps.** Notion and Obsidian templates, NotePlan, Logseq and other outliners, plus dedicated "bullet journal" apps. They gain search, recurring items and backups. They lose migration friction: copying is free, so the filter disappears.
- **E-ink**
  - **reMarkable, Kindle Scribe, Boox, Supernote:** dot-grid templates and hyperlinked PDF planners with tabs for index, months and days. The PDF links make the index and threading tap-to-jump.
  - **Handwriting recognition** varies by device.
  - **The Keeping Watch X4** is a reader, not a writing slate. It can show logs, not take handwriting.
- **Scanning.** Phone scans (Apple Notes, Google Drive, Microsoft Lens) give you a searchable archive, and the Companion app does this. Keeping Watch's printed pages add a Data Matrix and scan zones, so a photo can be filed automatically.
- **Hybrid pattern.** Calendar in an app, tasks and notes on paper. Or quick capture on the phone, migrated to paper at night.
- **Lost without paper:** the slow, deliberate rewrite, handwriting's engagement, and screen-free time.
- **Gained with digital:** alerts (which Keeping Watch deliberately avoids), search and portability.

## 16. Evidence and research

**No randomised or controlled study of the Bullet Journal Method itself was found** (search, Sept 2026). The evidence below supports its **components**.

| Study | Sample and design | Finding | Strength and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masicampo & Baumeister (2011), *J. Personality and Social Psychology*, doi:10.1037/a0024192 | Several lab experiments (Studies 1–5B) with student samples | Unfinished goals caused intrusive thoughts and interference. **Making a specific plan eliminated those effects**, even with the goal still unfinished | Moderate. Lab tasks, short time frames, student samples. Supports "write it down with a plan" (migration and scheduling) |
| Scullin, Krueger, Ballard, Pruett & Bliwise (2018), *J. Experimental Psychology: General* 147(1):139–146 | 57 healthy adults aged 18–30, randomised, overnight polysomnography | Writing a **to-do list** for 5 minutes at bedtime → fell asleep about 9 minutes faster (15.8 vs 25.1 min) than writing about completed tasks. More specific lists predicted faster sleep | Moderate. Small, young, healthy sample, one lab night. Supports the evening review that writes tomorrow's tasks |
| Sohal, Singh, Dhillon & Gill (2022), *Family Medicine and Community Health* 10(1):e001154 | Systematic review and meta-analysis of about 20 RCTs (PTSD, anxiety, depression) | Journaling interventions gave a small improvement over controls | Low to moderate. High heterogeneity, mostly expressive writing rather than planning logs |
| Morehead, Dunlosky & Rawson (2019), *Educational Psychology Review* | Replication of Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014) on longhand versus laptop notes | No consistent difference. Only small, non-significant effects favouring longhand | Weakens the popular claim that handwriting is proven better for memory |

**Claimed versus supported**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Writing tasks down reduces mental clutter | **Partly supported** (Masicampo & Baumeister; Scullin) |
| A bedtime to-do list helps sleep | **Supported** in one good RCT with healthy young adults |
| Journaling helps mood | **Some support**, a small effect, mostly other journaling styles |
| Handwriting beats typing | **Not established** |
| BuJo helps ADHD | **Anecdotal only.** Many first-person reports, including the creator's; no trials found |
| Migration improves prioritisation | **Plausible, untested** |

## 17. Benefits

1. One trusted place for everything, which cuts scattered notes. *Claimed*
2. Writing a plan quiets intrusive thoughts about unfinished tasks. *Evidenced* (lab studies)
3. An evening to-do list can help you fall asleep faster. *Evidenced* (one RCT)
4. Migration's rewrite friction weeds out low-value tasks. *Claimed*
5. Very cheap, private, offline and screen-free. *Claimed*, and obviously true
6. Adapts to changing needs month to month. *Claimed*
7. It builds a searchable life record through the index and archive. *Claimed*
8. Small mood and wellbeing gains from regular journaling. *Evidenced*, small and general
9. Can be a calm, mindful ritual. *Claimed*
10. Helps ADHD brains externalise working memory. *Claimed* (widely reported)

## 18. Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Comparison and perfectionism** ("my spreads are ugly") | Keep one pen and no decoration for the first month. Hide Instagram. Remember the method is the notation, not the art |
| **Set-up takes longer than using it** | Set up one month at a time and never pre-draw a year. Time-box set-up to 20 minutes |
| **Migration fatigue** (rewriting 40 tasks) | Strike more. If a task has moved 3 times, decide: do it now (under 5 minutes), schedule a date, or drop it |
| **Missed days lead to abandonment** | The Daily Log has no fixed pages, so just write today's date and carry on. No guilt pages |
| **Too many trackers** | Cap it at 3. Remove any you haven't filled for 2 weeks |
| **Forgetting to check the Future Log** | Tie it to the monthly set-up checklist. Or use Alastair columns, which are easier to scan |
| **Index abandoned** | Index only collections and months. Use threading for continuity |
| **Events buried in daily pages** | Copy dated events to the Monthly calendar the moment you log them |
| **Recurring tasks rewritten endlessly** | Put routines on a tracker or pre-printed list, not as daily `•` entries |
| **The notebook isn't with you** | Capture on a phone or sticky note and migrate at night (the Companion app pattern) |
| **Hand pain or fatigue** | Shorter entries, a thicker pen, pre-printed pages (see §19) |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD.**
  - The method was built for this. Keep it tiny: date, three `*` tasks, log, `X`.
  - Use Alastair-style columns so tasks aren't rewritten daily.
  - Add a "done" list to see progress.
  - Tie review to a fixed cue, like the kettle or the bus.
  - Don't make migration a big monthly event. Do it in 5-minute chunks.
- **Autism and sensory needs.**
  - Consistent, predictable layouts are better than monthly redesigns.
  - Use a fixed symbol set with a printed key.
  - Prefer low-contrast dot grid and a plain black pen. Avoid glossy or textured stickers if touch is aversive.
- **Chronic illness and low spoons.**
  - Log capacity (spoons) at the top of the day. Mark tasks with an energy cost.
  - Treat `>` as neutral, not failure.
  - Keep a "minimum day" list.
  - Log symptoms as notes under events to spot patterns.
- **Depression and anxiety.**
  - The evening to-do list can reduce bedtime rumination.
  - Keep a "small wins" note line.
  - Avoid long overdue-task lists: strike freely, and date a fresh start.
  - Mood trackers can help or can feed rumination, so watch which.
- **Trauma.**
  - Keep notes brief and factual. Put nothing in writing you'd fear someone finding.
  - Have a "safe to write" plan and a private place for the notebook.
  - Grounding collections (senses, safe people) can go at the back.
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia.**
  - Symbols do a lot of work. Allow your own abbreviations and icons.
  - Choose dot grid over lines if spacing is hard. Consider voice capture migrated later.
  - Use larger formats (8.5 × 11).
- **Low vision.**
  - Use a bigger notebook, a thick black pen (0.8 mm+) and high-contrast lined paper.
  - Choose large, distinct symbols: filled square versus open circle.
  - Use tactile tabs for the index and current month.
- **Motor issues or hand pain.**
  - Pre-printed pages remove drawing.
  - Use short entries, wide-grip pens and a flat-lying binding.
  - Consider a hybrid digital version for capture.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people.**
  - Chosen name on the cover. Privacy for medical notes.
  - HRT doses and appointments fit as events and tasks.
  - Collections for affirming moments, name-change paperwork and documents.
  - Be careful with anything that shows deadnames or legal details if found.
- **Shift workers.**
  - Date each Daily Log by **your** day, not midnight.
  - Log shifts as events on the monthly calendar.
  - Weekly spreads help more than daily ones, and sleep fits as a note or tracker.
- **Low income.**
  - Any $1 notebook works, as does printer paper in a folder.
  - Skip stickers and apps. Libraries have the book.

## 21. Combining

- **With the Alastair Method.** Swap the six-box Future Log for column dots, and use its context to-do or project board as collections. See [alastair-method.md](alastair-method.md).
- **With GTD.** BuJo becomes the capture and next-action layer. Contexts can be signifiers (`@phone`), and the monthly migration works as a weekly review done less often.
- **With habit and mood trackers.** A monthly tracker grid beside the Monthly Log.
- **With gratitude or "small good things".** A nested note each evening.
- **With expressive or reflective journaling.** Keep long writing in a collection, or a second notebook threaded from the Daily Log.
- **With time-blocking.** A column of hours beside the Daily Log for shift days.
- **With a printed planner** like Keeping Watch. Printed pages carry the calendar and check-ins. The BuJo notation and migration run inside them.

## 23. Glossary

- **BuJo.** Short for Bullet Journal; also a verb ("to bujo").
- **Bullet.** The symbol before an entry: task `•`, event `○`, note `–`.
- **Calendex.** Eddy Hope's calendar-index future log.
- **Collection.** Any page or set of pages on one topic, including the core logs.
- **Daily Log.** The running daily page, dated as you go.
- **Edition 2.** The 2020 official notebook with 120 gsm paper.
- **Event.** A dated occurrence, logged before or after.
- **Explore.** A signifier (eye) marking something to research.
- **Future Log.** A multi-month overview for items beyond this month.
- **Index.** The front table of contents, built as you go.
- **Inspiration.** A signifier `!`, usually on a note.
- **Key.** A reference page listing the bullets and signifiers.
- **Mental inventory.** The need / should / want list exercise.
- **Migration.** Rewriting open tasks forward (`>`) during review.
- **Monthly Log.** The calendar page plus task page for a month.
- **Nesting.** Indenting entries under a parent.
- **Note.** A non-actionable entry `–`.
- **Practice (the).** The reflective half of the method.
- **Priority.** A signifier `*`.
- **Rapid logging.** Short, symbol-tagged entries.
- **Reflection.** Regular review of intentions and tasks.
- **Scheduled.** A task state `<`, moved into the Future Log.
- **Signifier.** An extra mark left of a bullet that adds context.
- **Spread.** Two facing pages. In the community, any designed layout.
- **Sprint.** A small, time-boxed step toward a goal (book term).
- **System (the).** The notation and pages half of the method.
- **Task.** Something actionable `•`, done `X`.
- **Threading.** Writing the previous or next page number beside a folio to link split collections.
- **Topic.** A page's heading, used for the index.
- **Weekly Log.** An optional week overview spread (official from 2023).
- **Yearly Migration.** The new-notebook ritual.

## 24. FAQ

1. **Do I need the official notebook?** No. Any notebook works. Number the pages.
2. **Do I have to decorate it?** No. Decoration is a community hobby, not part of the method.
3. **What if I skip days?** Write today's date and continue. The Daily Log has no fixed pages.
4. **Daily or weekly log?** Start with daily. Add a weekly log if you need to see the week. Many people do weekly only.
5. **How far ahead should I set up?** One month. The Future Log holds the rest.
6. **What's the difference between `>` and `<`?** `>` moves a task forward to the next log or a collection. `<` sends it to the Future Log for a specific later month.
7. **What do I do with recurring tasks?** Use a tracker or routine list, not a daily `•` rewrite.
8. **Is it good for ADHD?** It was built by someone with ADHD, and many report it helps. No trials exist. Keep it minimal.
9. **What about appointments?** Put them on the Monthly Log calendar (and the Future Log if later). Many people keep a digital calendar too.
10. **Can I use it on a tablet?** Yes, with templates and hyperlinked PDFs, but migration friction mostly disappears.
11. **What goes in the index?** Collections and Monthly Logs. Not every daily page.
12. **When does a notebook end?** When it's full. Do a Yearly Migration then, or on 1 January.

### d. Proposed method layout: "Bullet Journal"
Ordered blocks for one click:
1. `sky` (Moon, sun and season): kept small, for the date anchor
2. `events` (Events)
3. `migrate` (Carried over), lines 3
4. `top` (Top priorities), label "Priority", n 3, shown as `*`
5. `bullets` (Quick bullets), key strip on, paper dots, fills the space
6. `review`: only "Tomorrow"
7. `body` (Writing space, dots): takes the rest

Care check-in stays optional; Shelbee can add `care` and `spoons`.

### e. Don't adopt

- **"Build as you go" blank pages for the whole book.** Keeping Watch's value is pre-printed, calm structure and scan zones. Blank books break `layout.json` mapping.
- **Decorative spreads, hand lettering and themed covers.** Busy ornament. They clash with the calm, low-stimulation design.
- **Colour-coded keys.** The book is black-and-white print. Use shape, not colour.
- **Threading in the footer.** The footer is a fixed scan zone.
- **Daily task counters or streaks on the X4.** They drift toward badges and pressure.
- **Emoji signifiers as font glyphs.** They'd create Type 3 fonts. Draw them as SVG.
