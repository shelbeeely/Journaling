# Passion Planner

A paper planner that starts with your dreams (the Passion Roadmap), breaks one down into dated steps, and gives every week a scheduled half and a blank half.

Research checked 2026-09-28. Points the sources couldn't settle are marked **(unverified)**.

## 2. Summary

Angelia Trinidad designed Passion Planner after college (2012) as "the one place for all your thoughts": an appointment calendar, journal, goal-setting guide and to-do list in one book. She funded it on Kickstarter in October 2013 (about $48,000 from about 2,000 backers), and the Compact edition raised $650,000 a year later (Our Story). The method starts with the **Passion Roadmap**. You brainstorm, against a timer, everything you want in 3 months, 1 year, 3 years and a lifetime. You circle one "GameChanger" in each timeframe, mind-map one of them into steps, give each step a due date, number the steps, and write them into your calendar. Each **month** has a Personal and a Work focus, people to see, places to go, a "Not To-Do List", project lists, and a **monthly reflection** with guided questions. Each **week** has hourly time slots (roughly 6 a.m.–10:30 p.m.), separate **Personal** and **Work** to-do lists, a weekly **Focus**, a quote and challenge, "**Good Things That Happened**", and a blank "**Space of Infinite Possibility**". The company also publishes 80+ free printables, including a **Year in Pixels** mood tracker, and runs a give-back programme. For Keeping Watch, the most portable ideas are the blank "infinite possibility" box, the Good Things list, the split Personal/Work lists (as Home/Shift), a monthly reflection page, and a gentle version of the Roadmap as a once-a-year back-matter spread.

### Cultural context
- **Timing.** It launched on Kickstarter in 2013, the peak of crowdfunded stationery, alongside the bullet-journal boom (BuJo went public the same year).
- **Audience.** It spoke to anxious twenty-somethings: "life anxiety", graduating into uncertainty. Its language (dreams, passion, GameChanger) is life-coach language on purpose; one campaign title was "The Life Coach That Fits in Your Backpack".
- **Give-back model.** Buy-one-give-one for students and nonprofits became part of the brand.

## 4. Philosophy and principles

### Stated goals
- Bridge "where you are and where you want to be" (product copy).
- Combine "structure with flexibility" to support mental health, productivity and personal growth.
- Make dreams concrete: "specific, actionable, measurable, and time sensitive" steps (Roadmap PDF).

### Core beliefs
1. **Start from dreams, not tasks.** Brainstorm without judging ("do not feel the need to be realistic or justify your dreams. Just write.").
2. **Prioritise ruthlessly.** One circled goal per timeframe; the GameChanger is "the goal that would have the most positive impact on your life right now."
3. **Break big into small, then schedule.** Mind-map, add specifics, number the steps, "make a date".
4. **Do one thing early.** "Strive to do at least one productive thing at the beginning of your day to get you closer to your goal."
5. **Balance work and life on the same page.** Separate Personal and Work lists and focuses, "to make sure that they are both prioritized."
6. **Leave room for the unplanned.** The Space of Infinite Possibility is a blank area on every week or day.
7. **Reflect monthly.** Look back at your priorities and adjust.
8. **Accountability.** "Extra credit: find a follow-up buddy."

### The problem it solves
- Goals that live in your head and never reach the calendar.
- Work swallowing personal life, or the reverse.
- Planners that hold appointments but not meaning.
- No habit of looking back.

### 5.1 Uncover Your Compass
- A core-values exercise at the front of current editions (product page).
- The exact prompts weren't in any source opened **(unverified)**.
- Its purpose is to anchor the Roadmap in values before goals.

### 5.2 Passion Roadmap
The steps, from the official 2020 PDF:
1. **Make a wishlist (5 min timer).** Four sections: *Lifetime*, *3 Years*, *1 Year*, *3 Months*, each with a date. "Imagine that whatever you write on this piece of paper will come true within the designated time frame."
2. **Prioritise (1 min).** Circle one goal per section that "would have the MOST positive impact on your life."
3. **Create your first Passion Plan (5 min).** Write your GameChanger in the centre box, write all the steps around it, and connect each step with a line.
4. **Add specifics.** Due dates: "how much, how many, or by when."
5. **Create a timeline.** Number the tasks in order.
6. **Make a date.** Write the tasks on their dates in the weekly layout.
7. **Work at it!** One productive thing at the start of each day.
8. **Repeat** for more goals.

- **Extra credit.** Find an accountability buddy. For a harder version, relabel the sections *5 Years / 1 Year / 3 Months / 1 Month*.
- **2024 blog version.** Steps become Wishlist, Prioritise, **Visualise** (Vision Board), Create a Passion Plan, Add Specifics, Strategise, Schedule, Work At It.

### 5.3 GameChanger
- The single highest-impact goal right now.
- It appears as a monthly "GameChanger breakdown" and as the Daily edition's "GameChanger Goal" line.

### 5.4 Monthly layout
Listed across the product page and the reviews:
- **This Month's Focus**, with separate Personal and Work boxes.
- **People to see**, **Places to go**, and learning (the product copy says "people, places, learning").
- **Not To-Do List**: "things you want to focus on not doing".
- **Personal and Work projects**, by priority and due date.
- A month calendar.
- **GameChanger breakdown** (a mind-map area at the bottom left, per reviews).

### 5.5 Monthly reflection
- The 2027 product page says **8 guided questions** plus habit completion checklists. A 2021 company post says **seven prompts** plus a Roadmap checklist.
- Questions that appear across sources (wording may vary by edition **(unverified)**):
  1. What was the most memorable part of this past month?
  2. What were the three biggest lessons you've learned in this past month?
  3. Review your planner for the past month and assess your priorities. Are you happy with how you spent your time? If not, what steps can you take next month to adjust them?
  4. How are you different between this past month and the month before it?
  5. What or who are you especially grateful for this past month?
  6. Name three things you can improve on this upcoming month. What concrete actions can you take to work towards these improvements?
  7. From 1–10, how do you feel overall about this past month?
- **The company's "6 ideas" for the reflection** (2021):
  - **Chart it:** graph the 1–10 ratings.
  - **Expand it:** rate areas of life.
  - **List it:** a running lessons list.
  - **Send it:** thank-you notes.
  - **Return to it:** check last month's three improvements.
  - **Celebrate it:** a yearly "I Did It List".

### 5.6 Weekly layout
- **Hourly timeline.** One column per day, about 6:00 a.m.–10:30 p.m. in half-hour steps (two reviews; one search summary says 6 a.m.–11 p.m.) **(exact range varies by edition)**.
- **Weekly Focus** (Personal and Work), tied to your Roadmap.
- **Personal To-Do** and **Work To-Do**, prioritised.
- **Weekly quote and challenge.**
- **Good Things That Happened**: gratitude for the week.
- **Space of Infinite Possibility**: blank (or dotted) space "for creativity", under the to-do lists.
- **"Today's Focus"** is listed in the 2027 copy, which suggests a per-day focus line **(unverified placement)**.

### 5.7 Daily layout (Daily edition)
- **Left page:** GameChanger goal, Work and Personal to-dos ("to make sure that they are both prioritized throughout the day"), an hourly calendar, and a Self-Care Practice ("incorporate a healthy habit into your daily life").
- **Right page:** a full-page **Space of Infinite Possibility**.
- The collection copy also mentions mood trackers and reflection questions.
- It's undated: "if you skip a few days here and there, you can pick right up where you left off."

### 5.8 Space of Infinite Possibility
- The signature element: blank space with no instructions.
- People use it for ideas, doodles, stickers, mind maps, journaling or scrapbooking.

### 5.9 Good Things That Happened
- A weekly gratitude box: "the good things that happened each week."
- It first appeared in the 2014 edition: "a place to jot down good things that happened" (Tech.co).

### 5.10 Year in Pixels (free printable)
- **The idea.** One square per day for a year, coloured by mood. It was created by Camille of the French blog *Passion Carnets* (no relation to Passion Planner) for 2016, inspired by Tim Urban's "Your Life in Weeks", and went viral by early 2017.
- **Passion Planner's version.** A free PDF in S (5.5 × 8.5 in), M (6.75 × 9.66 in) and L (8.5 × 11 in).
- **How to use it** (official steps):
  - "print full size at 100%";
  - "cut on the dotted lines so it will fit into your planner";
  - "tape along one side onto your blank pages" ("Washi tape works the best");
  - "assign colors to your moods and fill in each square daily".
- It includes a notes section.

### 5.11 Other free printables
- The Free Downloads collection lists 81 items, including:
  - a 1 Month Undated Planner PDF;
  - a 21-Day Gratitude Challenge and a 30-Day Journal Challenge;
  - a 52-Week Habit Tracker and a Book Tracker;
  - a Burnout Recovery & Rest Planning Guide;
  - a Monthly Reflection PDF and the Passion Roadmap PDF;
  - a bingo board and coloring pages.
- There are also "handwriting test" pages for choosing a size.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (about 60–90 minutes; can be split)
1. **Pick your edition.**
   - Weekly if your days are mostly routine.
   - Daily if you want room to write every day.
   - Undated if you often skip or start mid-month.
2. **Uncover Your Compass (15 min).** List your values. Circle 3–5.
3. **Passion Roadmap (about 15 min, timed).**
   - 5-minute wishlist across Lifetime / 3 Years / 1 Year / 3 Months.
   - 1 minute to circle one in each.
   - 5 minutes to mind-map your GameChanger.
4. **Add specifics and number the steps (10 min).**
5. **Make a date (10 min).** Copy the first 3–5 steps into this month's and this week's pages.
6. **Vision board (optional, 15 min).** Images, quotes, sketches.
7. **Yearly overview.** Write your 3-month GameChanger in this quarter's goal box.
8. **Habit tracker.** Choose 3–6 habits for the month.
9. **Month page.** Personal Focus, Work Focus, people, places, Not To-Do.

### First day
- **Morning (5 min).** Write one Roadmap step as today's first task. Block your fixed hours (work, classes, appointments).
- **Evening (5 min).** Tick what's done. Write one Good Thing. Doodle or write anything in the Space of Infinite Possibility.

### First week
- **Sunday (or Monday) planning, 15–20 min:**
  - Weekly Focus: one Personal, one Work.
  - Fill the to-do lists: 3–7 items each, starred priorities.
  - Block time for the top two.
  - Read the quote and challenge.
- **Daily:** one early productive step, tick things off, move unfinished items.
- **End of week:** fill in Good Things That Happened (3–7 items), and check off any Roadmap steps.

## 7. Daily practice

### Weekly edition (5–15 min a day)

| Step | When | Time | What | Prompt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Morning | 1 min | Look at today's column | "What's fixed today?" |
| 2 | Morning | 2 min | Choose one GameChanger step to do first | "What one thing today gets me closer?" |
| 3 | Morning | 2 min | Pick today's items from the Personal and Work lists | "Is this action getting me closer to my goals?" |
| 4 | During the day | — | Do the first step early; tick as you go | "One productive thing at the beginning of your day." |
| 5 | Evening | 2 min | Tick or move items; note times | — |
| 6 | Evening | 1 min | Add to Good Things That Happened | "What went well, however small?" |
| 7 | Any time | 0–10 min | Space of Infinite Possibility | "What's on my mind? Ideas? A doodle?" |

### Daily edition (10–20 min a day)
1. **GameChanger goal** for today (1 min).
2. **Work to-dos** and **Personal to-dos** (3 min).
3. **Hourly schedule** (3 min).
4. **Self-care practice**: pick one (water, walk, stretch, call a friend) (1 min).
5. **Mood check** (30 s).
6. Evening: tick, reflect, and use the right-hand page freely (5–10 min).

## 8. Rhythms

| Rhythm | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly** | Plan: Focus, to-dos, time blocks, read the challenge. Review: Good Things, move unfinished tasks. | 15–30 min |
| **Monthly (start)** | Month page: Focus (Personal/Work), people, places, Not To-Do, projects, GameChanger breakdown, habit tracker rows. | 20–30 min |
| **Monthly (end)** | Monthly reflection: 7–8 questions, a 1–10 rating, Roadmap checklist. Optional "6 ideas": chart the ratings, lessons list, thank-you notes. | 15–30 min |
| **Quarterly** | Revisit the 3-month section of the Roadmap. Did the 3-month GameChanger happen? Write a new one. Update the yearly overview's quarterly goal. | 20–30 min |
| **Yearly** | New planner: redo the Compass and Roadmap, and look back at the year-end reflection and "I Did It List". | 60–90 min |
| **Migration** | No formal migration symbol. Unfinished to-dos are rewritten on the next week by hand. | — |
| **Archiving** | Keep old planners. The Roadmap and reflections are the long-term record. | — |
| **Index** | No built-in index. Some people use tabs (sold separately) and label the blank pages. | — |

## 10. Worked examples

Generic sample person: works outdoor shifts, part-time art course.

### Sample Roadmap (excerpt)

```
LIFETIME: illustrate a picture book · live near water · steady health
```

## 16. Evidence and research

No study tests Passion Planner itself. Its parts map onto established research.

| Study | Sample and design | Finding | Relevance | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Locke & Latham (2002)**, *American Psychologist* | Review of 35 years of goal-setting research | Specific, challenging goals improve performance more than vague "do your best" goals. Feedback and commitment help. | Supports the Roadmap's "specific, actionable, measurable, time-sensitive" steps. | Mostly task performance in work and lab settings. |
| **Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006)**, meta-analysis | Implementation-intention studies | If-then plans that link a situation to an action help people follow through. | Supports "make a date": writing steps into specific days and times. | Effects vary by domain. |
| **Harkin et al. (2016)**, *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis | 138 studies, N = 19,951 | Monitoring progress improves goal attainment (d+ = 0.40), more so when progress is physically recorded or reported or made public. | Supports habit trackers, monthly reflection and the "follow-up buddy". | Heterogeneous studies. |
| **Emmons & McCullough (2003)**, *J Pers Soc Psychol* | Three studies, including daily logs and adults with neuromuscular disease | Gratitude listing improved some wellbeing measures, especially positive affect. | Supports "Good Things That Happened" and the monthly gratitude question. | Modest effects; mixed replications. |
| **Oscarsson et al. (2020)**, *PLOS ONE* | 1,066 people making New Year's resolutions, RCT, 12 months | Approach goals beat avoidance goals (58.9% vs 47.1%). *Some* support (62.3%) beat *extended* support (52.5%). | A caution: the Roadmap's very detailed planning may help or backfire. Keep plans light. The Not To-Do List is avoidance framing, so pair it with an approach goal. | Self-report; online Swedish sample. |
| **Lally et al. (2010)**, *Eur J Soc Psychol* | 96 people, 12 weeks | Habit automaticity took 18–254 days to reach 95%; one missed day didn't matter. | Supports monthly habit trackers and the Roadmap's "if you skip a day, make up for it" (though "making up" isn't needed for habits). | Small sample. |
| **Pennebaker (1997)**, *Psychological Science* | Review | Expressive writing is linked to health benefits. | Weak support for using the Space of Infinite Possibility or the right-hand daily page as a journal. | Different dose and format. |

**Claimed vs supported**

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Helps you reach goals | Plausible. The parts are evidenced; the product isn't tested. |
| Supports mental health | Claimed. Gratitude and reflection have modest support; planning can also raise stress for some people. |
| Balances work and life | Claimed. Not studied. |
| Doing one thing early builds momentum | Claimed. Consistent with habit research, but not tested as stated. |

## 18. Pitfalls

| Problem | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The Roadmap is done once and never looked at again | It's at the front of the book, out of sight | Copy your four GameChangers onto a sticky note inside the weekly pages. Tick the Roadmap box in each monthly reflection. |
| Overwhelm from a dense layout | Many boxes | Use only three parts: the time grid, one to-do list and Good Things. Leave the rest blank without guilt. |
| "Passion" pressure | Big-dream framing on a bad month | Rename it: "Next small thing". Set the Roadmap timer to 2 minutes and allow "rest" as a goal. |
| An overplanned GameChanger | Detailed plans create more ways to "fail" (Oscarsson 2020) | At most 5 steps, and only the next one gets a date. |
| Empty dated weeks after a slump | Dated editions show the gap | Buy undated, or cross out the gap with one line: "rest weeks". |
| The time grid doesn't fit shift work | 6 a.m.–10:30 p.m., day-shaped | Write the shift as one bar. Use the rest of the column for notes. For night shifts, relabel the hours. |
| Work and Personal lists feel artificial | No job, or blurred roles | Relabel: Home / Out, or Must / Want. |
| Heavy and expensive | Premium planner | The free 1-Month PDF, printables, or a DIY version in any notebook. |
| Decorating replaces planning | The aesthetic spiral | Set a 10-minute decorating cap; the plan goes first. |
| "Make up for skipped days" becomes guilt | Roadmap step 7 | Skip it. Research says one missed day doesn't break a habit. |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD.**
  - The time grid supports time-blindness; use colour blocks.
  - Keep one GameChanger visible (sticky note).
  - The Space of Infinite Possibility catches intrusive ideas.
  - Use undated editions to avoid shame over gaps.
  - Set phone timers for the timed Roadmap; they suit ADHD urgency.
- **Autism and sensory needs.**
  - Pick the Elite Black or other plain designs.
  - Skip the stickers and quotes.
  - Relabel prompts in literal terms.
  - A fixed weekly planning time.
  - Treat the Not To-Do List as a boundaries list (no phone calls after 8 p.m.).
- **Chronic illness and low spoons.**
  - A 3-month GameChanger can be "stable routine".
  - Replace the Work list with "Body / Care".
  - Pace with the time grid (rest blocks count).
  - The monthly 1–10 can track symptoms.
- **Depression and anxiety.**
  - Good Things That Happened is the most valuable part; do it even if nothing else.
  - Avoid the Roadmap in acute lows.
  - Use "three lessons" gently.
  - Reflection question 3 ("happy with how you spent your time?") can be harsh. Answer with "what I'd like more of".
  - The company offers a Burnout Recovery & Rest Planning Guide.
- **Trauma.**
  - The lifetime wishlist can surface grief; allow skipping it.
  - Keep reflection present-focused.
  - Private planners only (don't share pages with a buddy unless it's safe).
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia.**
  - Use the Large size.
  - Use icons and colour instead of words in the time grid.
  - Voice-record the reflection.
  - Mind maps suit visual thinkers.
- **Low vision.**
  - Large (7.8×11) or a digital version with zoom.
  - Bold pens, high-contrast highlighters.
  - Print the free PDFs enlarged.
- **Motor or hand pain.**
  - The digital version with a stylus, or type into the PDF.
  - Ticks, not words.
  - Fewer lists.
  - The daily edition's right page is optional.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people.**
  - The Roadmap can hold transition milestones by timeframe without forcing deadlines.
  - Relabel Work/Personal as "Me / World".
  - Use Good Things for euphoria moments.
  - Keep sensitive plans (appointments, names) in coded form if others might see the planner.
- **Shift workers.**
  - Relabel the hour lines.
  - Mark shift days with one bar.
  - Plan your "week" from your rotation, not from Sunday or Monday.
  - An undated weekly lets you start the week on your first shift.
- **Low income.**
  - The free printables and the 1-Month Undated PDF.
  - The Roadmap PDF is free.
  - Draw the weekly layout in any notebook.
  - The company's give-back programme donates planners to students and nonprofits (application routes not checked **(unverified)**).

## 24. FAQ

1. **Weekly or Daily?**
   Weekly if most days are routine and you like seeing the week. Daily if you want a page to write on each day. The digital bundles include both.
2. **Dated or undated?**
   Undated if you've ever abandoned a planner after a gap. Dated if you like the prompt of printed dates.
3. **Do I have to do the Roadmap?**
   No, but it's the heart of the method. Try the 1-minute version: one circled goal per timeframe, one next step.
4. **What goes in the Space of Infinite Possibility?**
   Anything: ideas, doodles, lists, mind maps, stickers, a journal entry.
5. **I don't have a "Work".**
   Relabel it: School, Home, Care, Out, Money.
6. **Where do I get the Year in Pixels?**
   It's a free PDF on passionplanner.com (email sign-up) in three sizes. Print at 100%, trim, and tape it into the blank pages.
7. **How many reflection questions are there?**
   Seven or eight depending on edition, plus a 1–10 rating and a Roadmap checklist.
8. **Is Passion Planner the Kickstarter from 2014?**
   The first campaign launched in October 2013 for the 2014 planner. The Compact (2015 planner) campaign ran in late 2014.
9. **What paper does it use?**
   Current planners use 120 gsm bamboo paper.
10. **Is there evidence it works?**
    Not for the product itself. The methods it uses (specific goals, scheduled steps, progress monitoring, gratitude) have research support.
11. **Can I use it on reMarkable or Boox?**
    Not officially listed. The PDFs generally open, but hyperlinks and colour may not work **(unverified)**.
12. **Is it good for shift work?**
    Partly. The hour grid is day-shaped. Relabel it, or use the undated weekly and plan by rotation.

### d. Proposed method layout: "Passion Planner"

This layout is not among the built-in method layouts yet (today: original, Bullet Journal daily, Hobonichi, Five Minute, Theme System). Ordered blocks for a one-click day layout:

1. `sky`
2. `events`
3. `focus` — Home / Shift (planned; until then a Lined notes block)
4. `top` — label "Next step", n = 1 (the GameChanger step)
5. `split` — Home / Shift to-dos, 3 lines
6. `timeline` — from 6 to 22, every 2 hours (off if a shift day uses `shift`)
7. `care` — the self-care practice lives here
8. `good` — 3 lines
9. `pixel` — Day pixel, 5 levels
10. `body` — Writing space, blank: the Space of Infinite Possibility

The fixed header, frame, SEND TO strip and page code stay unchanged. The week spread and month pages get Good Things (weekly), the monthly reflection and the Year in Pixels column as printed features, not day blocks.

### e. Don't adopt

- **"Passion" and "GameChanger" language.** It's hype and pressure. Use plain words: "Next step", "What matters".
- **Lifetime wishlist on a timer.** Too heavy for low-energy periods, and can surface grief. Offer 3 months and 1 year only, untimed, in the Keeper (the home-kept companion book).
- **"If you skip a day, make up for it the next day."** Guilt, and not supported (Lally 2010). Journalwright Studio never asks you to catch up.
- **Weekly quotes and challenges.** Adds reading and stimulation. Skip it.
- **Dense spreads.** Keep the calm, spaced look. Pick 3–4 elements, not all ten.
- **Colour-coded mood pixels.** The print is B&W and the X4 is 1-bit. Use patterns.
- **Any buddy or sharing feature on the X4.** Out of scope (nothing leaves the device except over its own hotspot, or an explicit sync you start to your own account, and there are no feeds).
