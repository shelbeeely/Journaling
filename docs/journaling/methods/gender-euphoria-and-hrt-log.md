# Gender euphoria journal and HRT log

> **Not medical advice.** This doc describes journaling and record-keeping. It never tells you what dose, route or schedule to use. Hormone decisions belong to you and your prescriber. Numbers quoted from clinical guidelines are there so you know what your clinic might track, not so you can change anything on your own.

Last checked: 2026-09-28. Uncertain points are marked **(uncertain)**.

## 2. Summary

A gender euphoria journal records the moments you feel right in your gender: being called your name, a sentence in your voice, a mirror glance, a stranger's "ma'am". Research defines gender euphoria as "a joyful feeling of rightness in one's gender/sex" and finds it comes from the body, from inside, and from other people. The HRT log sits beside it: date, dose ticked, route, injection site or patch position, labs over time, body changes, mood and side effects. Together they give you two things clinics rarely give back: a record of your joy, and a clean history you control. Keep it private, keep it short, and let the euphoria side be the one that grows.

## 3. History and origin

### Who created it

- **Gender euphoria** comes from trans communities, not clinics. Beischel, Gauvin and van Anders (University of Michigan / Queen's University) note the term "has been mobilized by some LGBTQ people to describe the powerfully positive emotions that can come from one's gender/sex", in contrast to "gender dysphoria", which comes from psychiatry.
- **The Gender Dysphoria Bible** (an open, community-written site at genderdysphoria.fyi, started around 2019 **(uncertain year)**) popularised the idea that "anything that can be a source of dysphoria has an equal and opposite euphoria".
- **HRT logs** have no inventor. They come from:
  - clinic self-injection guides (Fenway Health's Safer Hormone Injection Guide tells people to rotate sites and use a new needle every time);
  - primary care protocols that ask for regular bloodwork (Rainbow Health Ontario, Callen-Lorde, UCSF);
  - forum and zine culture: "HRT diaries", "transition timelines", monthly selfie grids, voice clips.

### Cultural context

- Trans health care was built around distress: you had to prove dysphoria to get care. Euphoria journaling is partly a push back: it records what's good, not just what hurts.
- ISTSS (2023) argues that "overemphasis on pathology perpetuates harmful stereotypes" and suggests asking "what makes you feel really good in your body?"
- Politics matter. In 2025–2026 some US states restricted trans care and ID changes. Paper records can be a lifeline (proof of history, continuity if a clinic closes) and a risk (outing). Both are real.

### Key quotes

- "A joyful feeling of rightness in one's gender/sex." (Beischel et al., participant-derived definition)
- "A little shiny gender breakthrough." (participant quote used as the paper's title)
- "You do not need dysphoria to be trans, because black ink on a black canvas isn't visible without close examination and a lot of light." (Gender Dysphoria Bible)
- "Use new needles and syringes every time you inject. This is the most important thing you can do to keep yourself from getting sick!" (Fenway Health injection guide)

## 4. Philosophy and principles

- **Joy is data.** Euphoria is not a bonus. Grant et al. found people currently feeling euphoria had 58% lower odds of high distress and 39% lower odds of past-year suicidal thoughts (cross-sectional; not cause and effect).
- **Your history belongs to you.** Clinics change, charts get lost, providers leave. A log you hold means you can show a new prescriber what happened.
- **Track to inform, not to police.** Body changes are slow and uneven. The log should make patience easier, not feed daily mirror checks.
- **Short beats complete.** A tick and a line every day is better than a long entry once a month.
- **Private by default.** Where the book lives, what it says on the cover, and who could read it are part of the method.
- **Not a gate.** You don't need dysphoria scores to deserve care. Scales here are for you.

**The problem it solves:** appointments are short and memory is bad. "When did I switch to patches?" "When did my voice first feel like mine?" "Were the headaches on injection day?" The log answers in seconds.

| Side effects and mood | Words to circle | Daily or dose days |

| Body changes timeline | Month rows: what you noticed | Monthly |
| Voice log | Minutes practised, exercise, one recording note | Practice days |
| Name and pronoun milestones | First time, who, where | When it happens |
| Appointment prep | Questions, changes since last visit, refills | Before each visit |
| Documents tracker | ID, name change, letters, insurance | When things move |
| Supplies and refills | Vials, needles, patches, sharps box, pharmacy | Weekly glance |

### Euphoria line

- One line. Past tense, specific: "Barista said 'thanks, ma'am'", not "people were nice".
- Beischel et al. found three kinds of euphoria. Tag each line:
  - **Body (external):** clothes, hair, body changes, voice.
  - **Inside (internal):** self-recognition, "this is me".
  - **People (social):** name, pronouns, being seen, community.
- Over time the tags show where your joy comes from. That's useful when you're low.

### Dysphoria scale (optional)

- Journalwright Studio already has a **Dysphoria** Scale preset: a 5-step scale from *quiet* to *loud*.
- Clinical scales are longer and made for research: UGDS-GS (18 items, 1–5 agree), GCLS (38 items, past 6 months). Don't take them daily. At most, some people take a validated scale every few months to see trends **(check licensing before copying items)**.
- Skip it on days it would make things worse. A blank is fine.

### Dose tick

- Tick box per scheduled dose. Time if it matters to you.
- Missed? Write "missed" and nothing else. No guilt ink.
- Changes in dose are written only when your prescriber changes them, with the date.

### Route and site

- **Injections (IM or subcutaneous):** Fenway's guide says to avoid injecting "in the same spot over and over" to avoid pain and scarring. Common sites it names: outer thigh (IM and SubQ), upper outer buttock (IM), stomach avoiding the navel (SubQ), back of upper arm (SubQ).
- A rotation row makes this easy: `L thigh · R thigh · L belly · R belly` and you circle the one used.
- **Patches:** many estradiol patches change twice weekly (RHO lists "2x/week"); some are weekly. Log the day on and the spot. Rotate spots per the product leaflet.
- **Gel:** log area and time (skin contact matters for others).
- **Oral/sublingual:** a tick is enough.

### Side effects and mood

- Words to circle keep it quick: *headache, tired, tender, hot flash, crampy, dizzy, calm, clear, flat, bright*.
- RHO notes some people on injections report cyclic symptoms (hot flashes, headaches, fatigue) and suggests clinicians may vary blood draw timing to see peak, mid-cycle and trough. Your log is how you'd notice the cycle.

### Lab table

- Columns: date · days since last dose (for injections) · test · result · unit · range used by the lab.
- Record the **unit** (pg/mL vs pmol/L) every time. RHO: 100–200 pg/mL ≈ 370–735 pmol/L.
- Note which **reference range** the lab printed. RHO warns ranges may reflect sex assigned at birth if your health card marker hasn't changed.
- Don't interpret. Bring it to your prescriber.

### Body changes timeline

- One row per month. Write what you noticed, not what you hoped.
- RHO (adapted from Hembree 2017) gives expected onset and maximum times. Use them as patience, not a checklist:

| Feminizing effect | Onset | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Softer skin, less oil | 3–6 months | unknown |
| Body fat redistribution | 3–6 months | 2–3 years |
| Breast growth | 3–6 months | 1–2 years |
| Less muscle mass | 3–6 months | 1–2 years |
| Thinner body/facial hair | 6–12 months | >3 years |
| Scalp hair loss stops | 1–3 months | variable |

| Masculinizing effect | Onset | Max |
|---|---|---|
| Skin oil/acne | 1–6 months | 1–2 years |
| Facial/body hair | 3–6 months | 4–5 years |
| Deeper voice | 6–12 months | 1–2 years |
| Periods stop | 1–6 months | n/a |
| Fat redistribution | 1–6 months | 2–5 years |
| More muscle | 6–12 months | 2–5 years |

- Estrogen does not change voice pitch. For trans women, voice change comes from training (see Voice log).

### Voice log

- SOC-8 has a full chapter on Voice and Communication. Speech-language pathologists work on pitch, resonance, intonation, voice quality, and nonverbal communication.
- Log: date · minutes · exercise (e.g. resonance, pitch glides, reading) · strain (none/some) · one note.
- Recording: file name or date only. Listen weekly, not daily.
- Self-rating: the TWVQ (30 items, 4-point scale) is used by clinicians to measure voice-related quality of life. A daily 1–5 "my voice felt like me" is enough for a journal.
- If your throat hurts, stop and note it. Strain is a reason to see a voice clinician, not to push.

### Name and pronoun milestones

- First time a friend used your name. First coffee order. First "she" from a stranger. First time you corrected someone and it went fine.
- These become the euphoria back-list you reread on bad days.

### Appointment prep

- Three sections: **Since last time** (changes, side effects, missed doses), **Questions** (max 3, most important first), **Need** (refills, letters, referrals).
- Bring the lab table and the last 3 months of timeline.

### Documents tracker

- ID, passport, name change order, birth certificate, insurance, letters from providers, dates submitted and received.
- Keep account numbers and case numbers in the **Keeper** (stays home), not the daily book.

### Supplies and refills

- Vial opened date, needles left, patches left, next refill date, sharps box fill level.
- Fenway: sharps go in a sharps box (or a hard plastic bottle, where local rules allow), never loose in the trash.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (20 minutes)

1. **Decide where the book lives.** Home only? Bag? Who else is in the home? (See §19 and the privacy list in §25e.)
2. **Pick a cover.** Plain. No "HRT" or "transition" on the spine if that's a risk.
3. **Write your current regimen on one back page,** copied from your prescription label: medicine, route, frequency. Date it. Don't add doses you haven't been prescribed.
4. **Draw the site rotation row** once, or use the printed one.
5. **Start the lab table** on a back page. Copy any past results you have, with dates and units.
6. **Start the milestones list.** Write any "firsts" you remember, even old ones.
7. **Pick your daily three:** euphoria line + dose tick + one scale (dysphoria *or* mood). That's it.

### First day

- Tick your dose. Write one euphoria line, even small: "Painted my nails and liked my hands."
- If nothing comes: write "looking" and leave it. Tomorrow will have one.

### First week

- Day 3–4: add the source tag (Body / Inside / People) to each line.
- Day 7: read the week's lines. Circle your favourite. That's your week's keeper.
- If it felt like homework, drop the scale and keep the line and the tick.

## 7. Daily practice

| When | Step | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Dose time | Tick. Injection or patch day: circle site, write time. | 20 s |
| Any time | Catch a euphoria moment: 3–6 words on the page or a note to copy later. | 30 s |
| Evening | Finish the euphoria line; tag B / I / P. | 30 s |
| Evening | Circle mood or dysphoria (optional). Circle side-effect words if any. | 20 s |
| Practice days | Voice: minutes + exercise + strain. | 30 s |

**Order of operations:** safety first (dose, site), then joy (euphoria line), then numbers (scales). Ending on the scale can pull the mood down, so some people do the euphoria line last. Try both.

**Prompts when stuck:**
- What did I wear that felt right?
- Who used my name today?
- What did my body do that I liked?
- When did I forget to be anxious about how I looked?

## 8. Rhythms

### Weekly (5–10 min)

- Reread euphoria lines. Star one.
- Count tags: mostly Body? Inside? People? No judgment, just notice.
- Voice: total minutes; listen to one recording.
- Supplies: needles, patches, refill date.

### Monthly (10–15 min)

- Body changes row: what you noticed this month.
- Optional: one photo in the same light and clothes. Many people do a monthly grid; if photos trigger checking, skip.
- Carry the starred euphoria lines to a "Moments I felt like me" back page.
- Journalwright Studio: the yearly Keeper book (no scan codes) is where sensitive facts (account numbers, case numbers) go.

### Quarterly (around labs)

- Before labs: note date and time of last dose (for injections, where in the cycle).
- After labs: fill the table. Write questions for your prescriber.
- Optional: one validated scale (e.g. GCLS subscales) if you and your clinician use it.

### Yearly

- HRT anniversary ("hormoniversary"): reread the year's milestones. Write a letter to next-year you.
- Copy the regimen history and lab table to the next book or the Keeper.
- Decide what to archive and what to shred (see privacy).

## 11. Variations and offshoots

| Variant | Who | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Euphoria jar / box** | Community (Tumblr, Reddit) | Moments on slips in a jar; reread on bad days. No tracking. |
| **Transition timeline** | Community (YouTube, Reddit r/transtimelines) | Photos at fixed intervals; public or private. Visual only. |
| **HRT diary / "hormone diary"** | Community blogs and zines | Narrative monthly entries about body and feelings. |
| **Injection log card** | Clinics (Fenway and others) | Date, site, lot number. Pure safety record. |
| **Voice journal** | SLPs and voice communities | Recordings + practice minutes; sometimes TWVQ ratings. |
| **Dysphoria diary** | Some therapists | Tracks triggers of dysphoria to plan coping. Can drift negative; pair with euphoria. |
| **Gender exploration journal** | Questioning people, therapists | Prompts about identity, clothes, names; no medical side. |
| **Nonbinary gender log** | Community | Tracks shifting gender feelings day to day (genderfluid "weather"). |
| **Detransition or pause log** | Community | Same structure; tracks changes after stopping. Neutral, not a failure log. |
| **Clinical outcome scales** | Researchers (GCLS, UGDS-GS, TWVQ) | Validated, long, periodic. Not journals, but can be copied into one with permission. |

## 12. Community practice

- **What people do:**
  - Monthly selfie grids and "X months on HRT" posts (Reddit, Instagram, YouTube).
  - Voice training apps plus a notebook of minutes and clips.
  - Hormoniversary rituals: cake, letter to self, reread.
  - Injection-day routines: same time, same playlist, log in a notes app.
  - Euphoria lists pinned on the fridge or in a phone.
- **Common customisations:** stickers for "first" moments; colour for each euphoria source; a patch/injection calendar on the fridge; lab screenshots taped in.
- **Aesthetic vs minimalist:** Aesthetic camps make spreads with trans flag colours and illustrated body changes. Minimalists use one line and a tick. Both work; minimalist is safer to hide.
- **Caution heard often:** timelines can drive comparison ("she's further than me at 6 months"). Many people keep timelines private for that reason.
- **Anecdotal, not evidence:** all of the above is community practice, not studied.

## 13. Official products and formats

Not applicable. There's no official euphoria journal or HRT log product. Some clinics hand out injection logs; some apps exist (see §15). Journalwright Studio already offers the HRT & body care and Dysphoria presets and a trans support back page.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- **Pen:** any that doesn't smear; gel pens bleed on thin paper.
- **Paper:** 80–100 gsm handles ballpoint and fine liner. Lab tables are easier on 8.5×11.
- **Ruler:** for lab table rows.
- **Stickers/stamps:** a small star or heart stamp for euphoria moments; a dot sticker for injection days.
- **Sharps box and a place for it** (not a journal supply, but log its fill).
- **Phone recorder** for voice; name files by date only.
- **A private spot** for the book (drawer, lockbox) if needed.

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

- **Apps:** medication reminder apps, voice training apps, notes apps, spreadsheets. Some trans-specific HRT tracking apps exist **(names and privacy policies change; check before use)**.
- **Risks of apps:** cloud sync, data sale, subpoena, device searches, notifications on lock screens that out you. Health data outside clinics often isn't covered by HIPAA in the US **(uncertain in detail; varies by state)**.
- **E-ink (reMarkable, Kindle Scribe, Boox):** handwriting with passcode; still synced to a cloud unless you turn it off.
- **Scanning:** Journalwright Studio scans pages by their SEND TO strip. HRT and lab pages should route only somewhere private, or not be scanned at all.
- **X4:** offline, no sync, nothing leaves except over its own hotspot. A good fit for dose ticks and euphoria counts.
- **Lost vs gained:** paper can't be hacked but can be found; apps can remind you but can leak. A hybrid (paper for words, X4 for ticks) keeps both private.

## 16. Evidence and research

| Study | Sample | Finding | Strength / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beischel, Gauvin & van Anders 2022 | 47 people (mean age 26.6; mostly white, college-educated, US/Canada; trans, nonbinary and cis) | Five themes: euphoria as "joyful rightness"; external, internal and social sources; the term comes from community; dysphoria as conflict; the euphoria–dysphoria link is debated | Qualitative, small, young, not diverse. Good for concepts, not prevalence. |
| Grant et al. 2024/2025 (Private Lives 3) | 1,359 Australian trans adults | 69% ever felt euphoria (21.6% currently). Current euphoria: 58% lower odds of high distress, 39% lower odds of past-year suicidal ideation. Community belonging and easy access to care linked to more euphoria. | Cross-sectional, single item, convenience sample, 2019 data. |
| Jones et al. 2019 (GCLS) | 789 (451 trans, 338 cis) | 38 items, 7 subscales, α = .95; measures gender congruence and life satisfaction over 6 months | Validated outcome measure; UK sample. |
| McGuire et al. 2020 (UGDS-GS), via Chen et al. 2023 validation | Chinese sample in validation | 18 items; dysphoria and affirmation subscales; α = .89 | Measures dysphoria, not euphoria. |
| Dacakis et al. 2013; Davies & Johnston 2015 (TVQ MtF) | 35 trans women in development **(per secondary reports)**; 5 in the 2015 content-validity study | 30 items, 4-point scale; items match what trans women say about voice | Small samples; widely translated. |
| Endocrine Society 2017 (Hembree et al.) | Guideline | Monitor every 3 months in year one, then 1–2×/year | Expert guideline. |
| WPATH SOC-8 2022 | Guideline | Chapters on hormone therapy, voice, mental health; supports informed decision-making | Expert consensus; contested politically, widely used clinically. |

**Claimed vs supported:**
- *Supported:* euphoria is common and goes with lower distress (association only).
- *Supported:* regular monitoring is standard care.
- *Claimed:* writing euphoria down increases it. Plausible (positive-event journaling in general has some support), but **no study tests euphoria journaling directly**.
- *Claimed:* an HRT log improves care. Plausible (better history), **not studied**.

## 17. Benefits

- Notices joy that would otherwise pass. **Claimed** (plausible, unstudied).
- Current euphoria is linked to lower distress and suicidal ideation. **Evidenced** (association, Grant et al.).
- Faster, clearer appointments. **Claimed.**
- Site rotation reduces pain and scarring. **Evidenced** as clinical advice (Fenway), not trialled here.
- Spotting cyclic symptoms to discuss dosing interval. **Claimed**, grounded in RHO guidance.
- A record that survives clinic changes. **Claimed.**
- Patience: seeing slow changes over months. **Claimed.**
- Community connection (milestones shared). **Claimed.**
- Voice practice consistency. **Claimed.**

## 18. Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| Mirror checking every day | Body timeline monthly only. No daily body notes. |
| Dysphoria scale drags mood down | Drop it. Keep the euphoria line. Or do the scale first, euphoria last. |

| Too much to fill | Daily three only: line, tick, one scale. |
| Nothing euphoric today | Write "looking". Or reread the milestones page. |
| Voice strain from over-practice | Log strain; stop on pain; see an SLP. |
| Grief when changes are slow | Monthly rows show progress; reread month 1. |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD:** tie dose tick to an existing habit (brushing teeth). One-line euphoria. Pre-printed site row; circle don't write.
- **Autism and sensory needs:** icons over words; same layout every day; injection-day checklist to lower demand; note sensory side of dressing (texture of clothes can be euphoric or awful).
- **Chronic illness and low spoons:** tick-only mode; euphoria can be one word; skip voice on low days.
- **Depression and anxiety:** euphoria first, scale optional; if the scale shows a long run of 5s, that's a reason to reach out (the Safety plan and Trans support pages in a Journalwright Studio book; a clinician or a line you trust also counts).
- **Trauma:** body pages can be hard. Skip photos and measurements. Tag "Inside" moments more.
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia:** circles, ticks, stars; voice memo instead of lines; larger ruling.
- **Low vision:** 8.5×11, thick lines, high contrast; X4 large text.
- **Motor or hand pain:** ticks only; X4 buttons; pre-printed words.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people:** this whole doc. Nonbinary people: tag lines without binary words; Grant et al. found nonbinary people reported euphoria less often, maybe because language and care fit them less **(interpretation, uncertain)**.
- **Shift workers:** log dose by "shift start" rather than clock time; patch days by weekday.
- **Low income:** any notebook works; free clinics (Planned Parenthood, community health centres) often do informed-consent HRT **(varies by state)**; needle exchanges give free needles and sharps boxes (Fenway).

## 23. Glossary

- **Gender euphoria:** a joyful feeling of rightness in your gender (Beischel et al.).
- **Gender dysphoria:** distress from mismatch between gender and body or social treatment; also a DSM-5 diagnosis.
- **Gender incongruence:** ICD-11 term (not a mental disorder in ICD-11).
- **HRT / GAHT:** hormone replacement therapy / gender-affirming hormone therapy.
- **Feminizing / masculinizing therapy:** estrogen-based (often with an anti-androgen) / testosterone-based.
- **Estradiol:** the main estrogen used; forms include oral, sublingual, patch, gel, injection.
- **Anti-androgen:** medicine that blocks or lowers testosterone (e.g. spironolactone, cyproterone).
- **IM / SubQ:** intramuscular / subcutaneous injection.
- **Site rotation:** changing injection spots to avoid pain and scarring.
- **Peak / mid-cycle / trough:** hormone level highest after a dose / halfway / lowest before the next.
- **Reference range:** the "normal" range a lab prints; may be for sex assigned at birth.
- **pg/mL, pmol/L:** units for estradiol; 100–200 pg/mL ≈ 370–735 pmol/L.
- **Informed consent model:** care where the person decides after education, without requiring a therapist letter (Callen-Lorde style).
- **WPATH SOC-8:** World Professional Association for Transgender Health, Standards of Care v8 (2022).
- **GCLS:** Gender Congruence and Life Satisfaction Scale.
- **UGDS-GS:** Utrecht Gender Dysphoria Scale – Gender Spectrum.
- **TWVQ / TVQ MtF:** Trans Woman Voice Questionnaire.
- **SLP:** speech-language pathologist.
- **Resonance:** how the voice sounds bright or dark, separate from pitch.
- **Hormoniversary:** anniversary of starting hormones.
- **Clocked / passing:** being read as trans / being read as your gender (loaded words; use your own).
- **Outing:** someone revealing you're trans without consent.
- **Sharps box:** hard container for used needles.
- **Keeper:** Journalwright Studio's yearly home book with no scan codes.

## 24. FAQ

1. **Do I need to be on hormones to keep a euphoria journal?** No. Euphoria comes from name, clothes, people, and inside recognition too.
2. **Should I track dysphoria at all?** Only if it helps. Some people find it validating; others spiral. Euphoria-only is a complete method.
3. **Can I use my log to adjust my own dose?** No. Bring it to your prescriber. The log makes that conversation better.
4. **What if someone finds it?** Plan for it: neutral cover, code words, home storage, sensitive facts in the Keeper, and nothing you'd be harmed by. See §25e.
5. **How often should labs be?** Guidelines commonly say about every 3 months in the first year, then 1–2 times a year (Endocrine Society; RHO uses baseline, 3, 6, 12 months, then yearly). Your clinic decides.
6. **Why write the time since my last injection on the lab table?** Levels differ at peak and trough; RHO notes clinicians may check mid-cycle or trough.
7. **Do photos help?** For some. Same light, same clothes, monthly. Skip if they cause checking.
8. **I'm nonbinary. Does this fit?** Yes. Use your own words for euphoria; scales like UGDS-GS were made gender-spectrum on purpose.
9. **Can estrogen change my voice?** Not pitch. Voice training (ideally with an SLP) does. Testosterone deepens voice over 6–24 months.
10. **Is this journaling proven to help?** Euphoria is linked to wellbeing. Writing it down specifically hasn't been studied.
11. **What if I stop or pause HRT?** Keep the same log. Changes after stopping are information, not failure.
12. **Where do I get crisis support?** Trans Lifeline (US) 877-565-8860 is peer-run and says it is "divested from police". Journalwright Studio books can include a Safety plan and Trans support page. For anything local, check your local services and your clinician.

### e. Don't adopt

- **Dose fields with numbers pre-filled.** Journalwright Studio shouldn't print or suggest doses. A tick is enough.
- **Validated scale items (GCLS, UGDS-GS, TWVQ) printed verbatim.** Licensing and misuse risk; they're for clinical use.
- **Photo grids or body measurement charts on day pages.** Feeds checking; also high outing risk if found.
- **Countdown or streak displays on the e-ink companion.** Pressure and badges are out of scope.
- **"Trans", "HRT" or "transition" on the cover or spine by default.** Outing risk. Let the person choose.
- **Scanning HRT/lab pages to a shared destination.** Route them to a private SEND TO symbol or don't scan.
- **Lab values on the X4 or in exported logs.** Not needed on-device; more exposure if the device is lost.

**Privacy checklist for this method (from EFF's security plan questions):**
1. What am I protecting? (history, name, meds)
2. From whom? (family, housemates, employer, police, border)
3. How bad if it's found?
4. How likely?
5. How much trouble am I willing to take? (neutral cover, lockbox, Keeper, code words)

## 26. Open questions

- Does writing euphoria down increase it, or just record it? No study yet.
- Is a single daily euphoria scale valid? Grant et al. used a single lifetime item; no daily measure is validated **(uncertain)**.
- Are euphoria and dysphoria two ends of one scale or two separate things? Beischel's participants disagreed.
- Why do nonbinary people report less euphoria in Grant et al.? Measurement, access or language?
- How much do private paper records help or hurt in hostile legal climates? No research found.
- Clinical-sample euphoria studies (e.g. Reisner et al. 2023, Psychiatry Research) weren't readable here; their findings aren't used.
- Best site rotation pattern for SubQ estradiol? Clinic advice varies **(uncertain)**.
