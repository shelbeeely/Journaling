# E-ink journaling and templates

Journaling on e-paper: writing tablets (reMarkable, Kindle Scribe, Boox, Supernote, Kobo Elipsa), the
hyperlinked PDF planner market that grew around them, the design limits of e-ink screens, calm technology,
and what all of it means for the Keeping Watch X4 companion.

## 2. Summary

E-ink journaling means writing by hand with a stylus on an electrophoretic screen that looks like paper,
reflects light instead of shining it, and holds its image without power. Around these devices a large
market of templates and hyperlinked PDF planners grew up: files with tabs and links that let you jump from
a year view to a month to a day. The screens are slow, greyscale or muted colour, and prone to "ghosting";
good e-ink design works with those limits by moving as little as possible, using outlines rather than big
black fills, and keeping navigation shallow. Calm technology (Weiser and Brown, 1995; Amber Case's eight
principles) gives the design language: stay in the periphery, ask for the least attention, work even when
it fails. For Keeping Watch the lesson is that paper stays the record and the X4 stays a quiet glance and a
button check-in, never a second journal that competes for attention.

## 3. History and origin

### Who and what

- **E Ink.** Electrophoretic "electronic ink" came out of MIT Media Lab work in the mid-1990s and E Ink
  Corporation (1997). Millions of microcapsules hold charged black and white particles in clear fluid; an
  electric field pulls one colour to the top. The display needs power only when the image changes
  (E Ink, "How it works").
- **Ubiquitous and calm computing.** Mark Weiser (Xerox PARC) wrote "The Computer for the 21st Century"
  (Scientific American, September 1991), arguing computers should fade into the background. With John
  Seely Brown he wrote "Designing Calm Technology" (21 December 1995), the founding text for calm design.
- **Amber Case** turned calm technology into eight practical principles and the O'Reilly book *Calm
  Technology: Principles and Patterns for Non-Intrusive Design* (2015). She later set up the Calm Tech
  Institute, which certifies products; the institute lists the reMarkable Paper Pro among certified
  products.

### Cultural context

Paper planners (Bullet Journal 2013, Hobonichi, Passion Planner) and iPad planners (GoodNotes, Notability)
created a buying habit: a new planner file every year. E-ink tablets promised the paper feel without the
glare, notifications and app-switching of an iPad. The hyperlinked planner market moved over, sold mostly
as instant PDF downloads. At the same time people tired of phones began looking for "dumb" or "calm"
devices; e-paper became a symbol of that.

### Key quotes

- Weiser (1991), opening line as widely quoted: "The most profound technologies are those that disappear."
  **(The Scientific American landing page did not show this line; the quote is from the full article.)**
- Weiser and Brown (1995): "A calm technology will move easily from the periphery of our attention, to the
  center, and back."
- Weiser and Brown (1995), on why the periphery matters: they call our connection to the world around us
  "locatedness," "the fundamental gift that the periphery gives us."
- Amber Case, principle 1: "Technology should require the smallest possible amount of attention."
- Amber Case, principle 6: "Technology should work even when it fails."

## 4. Philosophy and principles

**Stated goals of e-ink writing devices:** paper feel, focus, no distraction, long battery, one place for
all notes. reMarkable sells "distraction-free" as the core promise.

**Core beliefs behind calm technology:**

1. Attention is scarce; tools should ask for as little as possible.
2. Information can live in the periphery (a glance, a shape, a light) and come to the centre only when
   needed.
3. The right amount of technology is the minimum needed.
4. Tools should fail gracefully: a sleeping e-ink screen still shows the last useful image.
5. Tools should respect social norms and people's dignity.

**Problems e-ink journaling solves:**

- Glare and blue light at night (reflective screen; front lights are optional and dim).
- Too many notebooks: one device holds years of pages.
- Search and backup: some devices convert handwriting to text.
- Distraction: no feeds, no browser (on most), no notifications.

**Problems it creates:** cost, charging, lock-in to one brand's format, the temptation to "optimise" the
planner instead of using it, and loss of the physical archive.

### 5.1 Devices

- **reMarkable 2** (2020): black and white, no light. **Paper Pro** (2024): 11.8" Canvas Color with a
  reading light. **Paper Pro Move** (2025): 7.3" colour, reading light, "up to 2 weeks" battery, from $449.
- **Kindle Scribe** (2022, refreshed 2024): 10.2", 300 ppi, warm light, "18 provided templates" for
  "notebooks, journals, and lists," AI summaries of up to 15 pages (2024).
- **Boox** (Onyx): Android-based, so any app runs, which is both power and distraction. Firmware 4.0
  (January 2025) added file-based notes, PNG templates and clickable PDF hyperlinks.
- **Supernote** (Ratta): closed, calm system. Custom templates are PNG/JPG/WEBP at 1404×1872 (A5 X, A6 X,
  A6 X2 Nomad) or 1920×2560 (A5 X2 Manta), dropped in the `MyStyle` folder.
- **Kobo Elipsa 2E** (and Sage, Libra Colour with stylus): Basic notebooks (free drawing, backgrounds,
  export PNG/JPEG/PDF) and Advanced notebooks (write between lines, convert to text, export docx/text/HTML).
- **Xteink X4**: 4.3", 220 ppi, 114×69×5.9 mm, 74 g, 650 mAh, about 14 days at 1–3 h/day, no touch, no
  light, side buttons, ESP32, about $69 (Good e-Reader review, March 2026). Not a writing device. The
  **X4 Pro** adds a touchscreen, front light and 1100 mAh battery (from $99).

### 5.2 Templates

A template is a background layer: lines, grid, dots, a planner layout. On most devices you cannot write
"into" fields; you write on top. Built-in templates are usually PNG-like bitmaps at the panel's native
resolution. Custom template support differs a lot by maker (see the table in section 15).

### 5.3 Hyperlinked PDF planners

One PDF with hundreds of pages (a 2026 Kindle Scribe daily planner from Templatables lists "over 700
pages" and "1,000's of hyperlinks"). A tab strip on every page links to year, months, weeks and days.
You write with the device's annotation layer. Strengths: navigation like a real planner, cheap, works on
any PDF-capable device. Weaknesses: files are huge and slow to open, you cannot add pages inside a day
without breaking links on some devices, handwriting on PDFs often can't be converted to text, and every
year you buy (or regenerate) a new file.

### 5.4 Refresh modes and ghosting

Electrophoretic particles move physically. A **full refresh** flashes black and white to reset every
particle (about 2 s on black-and-white panels) and removes ghosting. A **partial (fast) refresh** moves only
changed pixels in well under a second but leaves some particles part-way, so faint traces ("ghosts")
build up. Waveshare's guidance: "perform one full refresh after every 5–10 partial refreshes or at regular
intervals such as once per hour." Colour panels are far slower (15–30 s on some hobby panels). Particles
are slower in the cold, with many panels rated down to about 0°C.

### 5.5 Sleep screen

Because e-ink is bistable, whatever is on screen when the device sleeps stays there at zero power. Readers
use this for covers or art. The X4 companion uses it for today's page.

### 5.6 Export and sync

Kindle Scribe shares notebooks by email link (a PDF, plus a TXT if converted; link expires after 7 days).
Kobo exports PNG/JPEG/PDF or docx/text/HTML. reMarkable syncs through its cloud; Supernote and Boox offer
their own clouds plus local transfer. The X4 companion stays local: files go out only over its own hotspot.

## 6. Setting it up

### First setup (writing tablet)

1. Pick one device and one notebook app. Resist installing three planners.
2. Choose paper: dots or lines. Set it as default if the device allows.
3. Make four notebooks at most: *Daily*, *Collections*, *Archive*, *Inbox*. Or import one planner PDF.
4. If using a planner PDF: import it, then **duplicate the blank file** and keep the copy as a master so you
   can restart.
5. Set sleep timeout short (1–5 min) and the sleep screen to something calm.
6. Turn off Wi-Fi and cloud if you do not need them; turn on a weekly export instead.
7. Decide a backup day (e.g. Sunday) and where backups go (a computer folder, not only the maker's cloud).

### First day

- Morning: open today's page from the planner tab (or add a dated page). Write three lines. Close.
- Evening: tick what happened, one line of reflection.
- Do not customise anything on day one.

### First week

- Day 3: notice friction. Too many taps to reach today? Add a bookmark or move to a simpler file.
- Day 5: try one refresh setting change if ghosting bothers you (most devices have a "full refresh every N
  pages" option).
- Day 7: export the week as PDF. Look at it on a computer. Decide whether this is better than paper for you.

### First setup (Keeping Watch X4, no pen)

1. Build the month packs and copy `/kw/` to the SD card (`tools/export_pack.py`).
2. Set the clock from the Wi-Fi page.
3. Put the X4 somewhere you pass daily (fridge with the magnet ring, bedside, phone back).
4. Let it sleep. The screen shows today. That is the whole setup.

## 7. Daily practice

A sample e-ink journaling day, 6–12 minutes total:

| When | Step | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Wake | Glance at the sleep screen (X4) or open today's page: date, moon, events. | 10 s |
| Morning | Top 3 and one line "how I feel." On the X4: press Confirm, tick meds. | 1–2 min |
| Midday | Quick bullets as things happen. Each tap-to-open costs a wake; keep it to 2–3 times. | 1 min |
| Evening | Tick care boxes, circle mood/anxiety/spoons, one or two lines of reflection. | 3–5 min |
| Before sleep | Close the notebook so the device sleeps on a calm screen. | 10 s |

**Order of operations on a tablet:** open (from a bookmark, not by scrolling), write, never reorganise
during the day, close. Reorganising is a weekly job.

**Prompts that suit e-ink's slow screen:** short, one-line answers. "One word for today." "What drained me?"
"What helped?" "Tomorrow's first step."

## 8. Rhythms

- **Weekly (10 min):** export or back up; duplicate next week's pages if your template needs it; clear
  the inbox notebook; do a full refresh or two if the screen looks muddy.
- **Monthly (20 min):** archive last month's notebook (rename `2026-10 Daily`); export PDF; review
  tallies; for Keeping Watch, open X4 "This month" and copy the numbers into the Keeper's Closing page.
- **Quarterly:** check storage space, nib wear, battery health; update firmware only if something is
  broken (updates change layouts and break templates, e.g. Boox 4.0 changed how templates apply).
- **Yearly:** buy or generate next year's planner; export the whole year to PDF and store it in two places;
  decide whether the device still earns its place.

**Index upkeep:** hyperlinked planners replace the index with tabs. Plain notebooks need a page 1 index or
the device's search (only works on converted text on most devices).

## 11. Variations and offshoots

| Variant | Who | How it differs |
|---|---|---|

## 12. Community practice

- **What people do:** most e-ink journalers end up with a very plain setup (one dot notebook, dated
  headings) after a phase of buying planners. Planner collectors are a real sub-community; many admit to
  "planner hopping."
- **Common customisations:** darker grids (default greys vanish on some panels), left-hand tab rails for
  left-handers, "no-date" undated planners so a missed week does not waste pages, Monday vs Sunday starts,
  simplified "minimal" versions with fewer links.
- **Refresh tweaks:** Boox users switch refresh modes per app; readers set "full refresh every N pages";
  CrossPoint exposes refresh cadence as a setting.
- **Aesthetic camp:** colour Kaleido/Gallery devices, digital stickers, handwritten headers. Colour e-ink is
  muted and slow, so this camp often still prefers iPads.
- **Minimalist camp:** Supernote and reMarkable 2 users, black and white, one notebook, no cloud.
- **Friction points people report:** large PDFs lag; links break when pages are inserted; templates reset
  after firmware updates; nibs wear; export is clumsy; handwriting search is unreliable.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- **Stylus:** EMR pens need no charging (reMarkable, Scribe, Supernote, most Boox). Kobo Stylus 2 is active
  (rechargeable).
- **Nibs:** felt nibs give more friction and wear faster; ceramic or hard nibs last longer. Budget for spares.
- **Screen film:** some add matte film for more friction; it slightly lowers contrast.
- **Folio:** protects the screen; magnetic covers wake/sleep the device.
- **Computer tools:** a PDF editor (for making templates), an image editor that exports PNG at the device's
  native size, and a backup folder.
- **For the X4:** a microSD card, the magnet ring, a USB-C cable. No pen.
- Not needed: stickers, washi, stamps, rulers (though digital sticker packs exist).

## 16. Evidence and research

| Study | Sample | Finding | Strength and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benedetto et al. 2013, *PLOS ONE* | 12 adults, 3 sessions of about 73 min | LCD (Kindle Fire HD) caused more visual fatigue than e-ink (Paperwhite) or paper; e-ink similar to paper; lower blink rate on LCD. | Tiny sample, one novel, old devices. Supports "e-ink is easier on eyes than LCD," not a health claim. |
| Umejima et al. 2021, *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience* | 48 students, 3 groups of 16 | Paper notebook group wrote schedules faster, was more accurate on simple recall, and showed more hippocampal/precuneus activation at retrieval than tablet (stylus) or phone groups. | Small, one task, 1-hour delay, Japanese students. Suggests paper may help memory over tablet writing; tablet here was not e-ink. |
| Mueller and Oppenheimer 2014 | 3 experiments, students | Longhand notes beat laptop notes on conceptual questions. | Famous, but see next row. |
| Morehead, Dunlosky and Rawson 2019, *Educational Psychology Review* | Direct replication plus eWriter group | No consistent differences between longhand, laptop, eWriter, or even no notes; meta-analysis showed small nonsignificant effects favouring longhand. | The one study with an eWriter group. Undercuts strong handwriting claims. |
| Weiser and Brown 1995; Case 2015 | Design writing | Principles, not trials. | No outcome evidence; widely used in HCI. |

**Claimed vs supported:**

- "E-ink is easier on the eyes than a phone": some support (Benedetto), small.
- "Writing on e-ink is as good as paper for memory": not settled. Umejima suggests paper wins over tablet
  writing; Morehead found no clear eWriter penalty.
- "E-ink reduces distraction": plausible by design (no notifications), not directly tested for journaling.
- "E-ink journaling improves wellbeing": no direct evidence. Benefits come from the journaling method used.

## 17. Benefits

- Less glare and eye strain than phones and LCD tablets. **evidenced (small)**
- Paper-like handwriting with unlimited pages. **claimed**
- Fewer distractions than a phone or iPad. **claimed**
- Search and backup (when text-converted or exported). **claimed**
- Long battery, readable in sunlight (reflective screen). **evidenced by physics (bistable, reflective)**
- Sleep screen shows useful information at zero power. **evidenced by physics**
- Cheaper than buying many paper planners over years. **claimed (depends on device cost)**
- One object instead of a stack of notebooks. **claimed**

## 18. Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Why people quit | Concrete fix |
|---|---|---|
| Planner hopping | Buying and setting up files replaces journaling | One file per year, bought or made before 1 December; no new files mid-year. |
| Too many taps to today | Friction kills the habit | Bookmark today or use a one-notebook-per-month setup. |
| Ghosting and muddy pages | Looks dirty, feels broken | Turn on full refresh every 5–10 pages; avoid dark templates. |
| Links break after inserting pages | Planner feels fragile | Use planners with blank "extra notes" pages already built in, or plain notebooks. |
| Lock-in | Notes trapped in one format | Monthly PDF export to your own folder. |
| Battery dead when needed | Device unusable; paper never is | Charge on a fixed day; keep a paper fallback page. |
| Firmware changes templates | Layout resets | Delay updates; keep template files backed up. |
| Device becomes the hobby | Tweaking instead of writing | Timebox customisation to the monthly review. |
| Cost | $300–$600 up front | Start with paper; buy used; or use a reader like the X4 as a companion only. |
| Cold hands, cold screen | Outdoor shift work, slow refresh near 0°C | Keep the device in an inside pocket; write on paper outside. |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD:** e-ink removes app-switching, which helps. The risk is hyperfocusing on planner setup. Keep
  navigation to one tap; use tally counters rather than long forms. The X4's one-press check-ins suit
  "do it in the moment."
- **Autism and sensory needs:** reflective screens with no flicker suit light sensitivity. Full refreshes
  *flash*; choose devices that let you reduce full-refresh frequency, or accept a flash only at sleep (the
  X4 companion does this). Consistent layouts every day reduce load.
- **Chronic illness and low spoons:** a device beside the bed that shows today without opening anything is
  a real saving. Button check-ins take seconds. Large tablets can be heavy (Paper Pro is large); smaller
  (7") or the X4 weighs little.
- **Depression and anxiety:** avoid planners with streaks or empty-day guilt; undated pages help. Calm
  sleep screens beat notification pings.
- **Trauma:** privacy matters: device lock, no cloud, local exports. A physical notebook can be found; a
  locked device less easily, but cloud accounts can be subpoenaed or breached.
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia:** text conversion can help or frustrate; a clear sans font on sleep screens and
  large line spacing help reading. Handwriting on glass can feel slippery: try felt nibs or film.
- **Low vision:** e-ink contrast is lower than good paper; 300 ppi Scribe and zoom help; the base X4 has no
  front light, so it needs good room light. Use large type and heavy (not thin) lines.
- **Motor or hand pain:** styluses with thicker grips; pressure settings; buttons (X4) need no fine motor
  aim. Long-press actions should be tolerant (about 1 s).
- **Trans and gender-diverse people:** privacy of HRT logs and dysphoria notes; device lock and local-only
  storage; no account names shown on the sleep screen.
- **Shift workers:** night-friendly (no glare), but the "day" boundary matters: the X4 redraws at 12:31
  a.m., which is mid-shift for some. A configurable day-start hour would help (see 26).
- **Low income:** devices are expensive; paper is cheaper. Used Kindles or the $69 X4 as companion are the
  cheapest e-ink routes. Free templates exist for every device.

## 23. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| E-ink / e-paper | Reflective display where charged particles move to form the image. |
| Electrophoretic | Moving charged particles with an electric field. |
| Bistable | Holds an image with no power. |
| Full refresh | Flashing update that resets all pixels; clears ghosting. |
| Partial / fast refresh | Update of changed pixels only, no flash; builds ghosting. |
| Half refresh | Keeping Watch X4 term: a screen-change update between fast and full. |
| Ghosting | Faint leftover image from earlier screens. |
| A2 mode | A fast 1-bit waveform used for scrolling/pen input on many devices. |
| Waveform | The voltage sequence the controller uses to move particles. |
| 1-bit | Each pixel is black or white only. |
| 16-grey | Four bits per pixel: 16 shades. |
| Dithering | Faking grey with patterns of black and white dots. |
| ppi | Pixels per inch. |
| EMR | Electromagnetic resonance stylus (no battery). |
| Template | A background image or page layout you write over. |
| Hyperlinked PDF planner | A PDF with internal links (tabs) between pages. |
| Workbook | reMarkable term: a guided multi-page PDF. |
| MyStyle | Supernote folder for custom templates. |
| Sleep screen | Image shown while the device sleeps. |
| Front light | Edge light that shines onto the screen surface. |
| Calm technology | Design that uses the periphery and asks for minimal attention. |
| Periphery | What you notice without focusing on it. |
| Locatedness | Weiser and Brown: feeling connected to the world around you through the periphery. |
| CrossPoint | Open-source firmware for Xteink readers. |
| Bridge | Keeping Watch file (`/kw/checkins.txt`) that sends day-page blocks to the X4 as check-ins. |

## 24. FAQ

**1. Is e-ink better for my eyes than my phone?** Small studies say reading on e-ink causes less fatigue than
LCD and about the same as paper. It is not a treatment for anything.

**2. Is writing on e-ink as good as paper for memory?** Unclear. One fMRI study favoured paper over tablet;
a replication with an eWriter group found no clear differences. Pick what you will actually use.

**3. Why do my pages look dirty?** Ghosting from partial refreshes. Turn on a full refresh every 5–10 pages.

**4. Can I use my own templates on a reMarkable?** Officially the route is a PDF notebook (or Connect's
template feature). Supernote and Boox take PNG files directly.

**5. Why is my hyperlinked planner so slow?** Hundreds of pages and thousands of links. Split it by quarter
or use a simpler version.

**6. Can the X4 replace my paper journal?** No, by design. It has no pen or touch. It shows today, takes
button check-ins, and adds up the month.

**7. Why does the X4 sleep screen flash when it goes to sleep?** It uses a full refresh before sleeping so
the image that stays for hours is clean and ghost-free.

**8. Does colour e-ink help journaling?** Colours are muted and refresh is slower. For a calm black-and-white
system it adds little.

**9. What happens to my notes if the company goes away?** Export PDFs regularly. The X4 stores plain text and
CSV on its SD card, so nothing depends on a company.

**10. Should I use AI summaries on my journal?** Keeping Watch does not; journals are private and the
firmware has no generative AI by rule.

### 25b. X4

The X4 is a 480×800 (portrait) 1-bit panel on an ESP32-C3 with no PSRAM, about 380 KB RAM, one 48 KB
framebuffer, seven buttons, no touch, no light, weeks per charge. The firmware already follows most of
what the research recommends. This table lists what fits and what to watch.

| Idea | Where | Fit | RAM, scope and calm notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Refresh policy: fast for moves, half for screen changes, full on sleep, half every 8th fast** | Refresh (all screens) | high, **built** | Matches Waveshare's "full every 5–10 partials." Keep it. Don't expose it as a setting unless ghosting shows up in the hardware pass. |
| **Sleep screen = today** | Sleep screen | high, **built** | Calm tech principle 3 (periphery) and 6 (works when it fails: the image persists when the battery dies). |
| Stale-day marker when the clock is unknown or the redraw was missed | Sleep screen | high | Today shows "Clock not set" already; add a small "as of Thu 1" line if the date is more than one day old so an old page never passes as today. Tiny RAM. |
| **Avoid large black fills and inverted bars** | All screens | high, **mostly built** | Big blacks ghost and feel loud. Selection as a thin outline, not an inverted row. Audit Support and Menu for any inverse blocks. |
| **Custom check-ins from the day page (bridge)** | Check-in item kinds | high, **being added** | toggle/scale/count/dots; 16 custom slots; static arrays; no new screens. |
| Group headers from `@group` lines | Check-in screen | high | Plain small caps rule, same as paper block titles; keeps long lists scannable. |
| Page the check-in list instead of scrolling | Check-in screen | high | Scrolling on e-ink = many partial refreshes. Show one page of items (about 8), Left/Right on a header row turns pages. |
| "Quiet" sleep screen option: date, moon, one line only | Sleep screen, Wi-Fi page setting | med | For high-sensory days. One flag in `me.txt` or a settings file; no new RAM. Must not hide Support. |
| Personal "On this day" from own logs (e.g. "A month ago: mood 4, outside") | Today screen footer (not sleep) | med | Reads last month's CSV line for the same date; ≤1 KB. Calm only if neutral and optional; never compare or score. Needs Shelbee's yes. |
| One printed prompt per day on the sleep screen | Sleep screen | med | Prompt text comes from the pack (made by the journal build), rotating; no AI, no network. Low RAM. |
| Week view in "This month" (seven rows of done counts) | This month screen | med | Uses existing `doneByDay[]`; dots not bars (less black). |
| Battery low: a calm "charge me" page, drawn once | Sleep screen | high | Draw with full refresh while there is still power, so a flat battery leaves a clear message instead of a stale day. Not a notification: nothing blinks or repeats. |
| Clear to white before long storage | Menu → "Put away" | low | Waveshare advises storing panels on white to avoid burn-in ghosts. Rare use; one menu line. |
| Cold-weather note | README / Support | low | Particles slow near 0°C (Spokane winters, outdoor shifts). Document "keep it inside a pocket," no code. |
| Day start hour for shift workers (redraw at e.g. 4:31 a.m.) | Wi-Fi page setting | med | Replaces fixed 12:31 a.m.; affects `nextLocalMidnight`. Check-ins after midnight would still belong to "yesterday." Needs Shelbee's yes. |
| Large-text mode | All screens | med | Bitmap fonts are generated; a second size set costs flash, not RAM. Helps low vision and no-front-light use. |
| Button map card on the Wi-Fi page and in print | Wi-Fi page | high | Same icons as paper; no hidden gestures besides hold-Back-for-Support. |
| Keep navigation two levels deep | Menu | high, **built** | Hyperlinked-planner lesson: depth costs taps and refreshes. |
| Show "logged" state on Today (e.g. `CARE [x][x][ ]`) | Today screen | high, **built** | Periphery, not a badge: no counts in circles, no red. |
| Upload a template/background image | Wi-Fi page | low | Not useful: X4 can't write. Skip. |
| Handwriting, touch, colour, AI summaries | none | **don't** | Out of scope; see 25e. |
| Wi-Fi only on request, auto-off | Wi-Fi page | high, **built** | Calm tech principle 7 and privacy. |

**Calm checklist for any new X4 screen** (from Case's principles):

1. Can it be read in one glance from arm's length?
2. Does it ask for anything? If yes, is it the smallest possible ask (one press)?
3. Does it change on its own? (Only the midnight redraw may.)
4. Does it still make sense if the battery dies right now?
5. Does it use more black than the Today screen? If so, why?
6. Does it add a number that invites comparison or streak guilt? Remove it.

### 25e. Don't adopt

- **Handwriting or touch on the X4:** no digitiser; paper is the writing surface.
- **AI summaries or "refine handwriting"** (Kindle Scribe style): firmware rule, and journals are private.
- **Cloud sync of check-ins:** nothing leaves the device except over its own hotspot.
- **Streaks, badges, progress rings:** they turn care into performance.
- **Colour or greyscale photos on the sleep screen:** 1-bit dithered photos look noisy and ghost.
- **Hyperlinked mega-PDFs in the X4 library:** a 4.3" screen can't show letter-size pages usefully; EPUB is
  the right format.
- **Yearly planner files as a product:** the monthly book already does this better in print.
- **Frequent timed redraws (clock, weather):** costs battery and breaks the calm glance.

- Whether a 1-bit panel can do a reliable 4-grey mode on the X4 controller (CrossPoint may know).
