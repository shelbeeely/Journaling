# Planner, sticker and deco culture

The hobby world around decorated paper planners: Erin Condren, Happy Planner, Kikki.K, Plum Paper and hundreds of small
sticker shops; washi tape, stamps and "plan with me" videos. This doc describes the culture honestly, including its
costs and sensory load, and pulls out what a calm, black-and-white system like Journalwright Studio can borrow: functional
icons, sticker-sized spaces and printable sheets.

## 2. Summary

Planner culture treats a paper planner as both a tool and a craft. A week is planned, then decorated with stickers,
washi tape and stamps, often on camera. Stickers split into **functional** (icons, checklists, boxes, headers that
carry information) and **decorative** (themed art, "kits" that dress a whole week). A small-business economy of Etsy
shops, subscription boxes and brand launches feeds it. People say decorating calms them, makes planning something to
look forward to and gives them a creative outlet; qualitative studies of bullet journalers back the craft-and-reflection
part (Tholander & Normark 2020; Ayobi et al. 2018). The critiques are cost, consumerism, perfectionism, decorating as
procrastination, and visual overload for sensory-sensitive people. Journalwright Studio can take the functional core without
the colour and clutter: a consistent black-and-white icon set, empty sticker-sized spaces, and an optional printable
icon sheet, all kept away from the scan zones.

## 3. History and origin

### Who created it

No one. It grew from scrapbooking (1990s–2000s), Filofax and ring-binder culture, Japanese stationery (Hobonichi,
washi tape), and teacher planners, then fused on blogs, Instagram and YouTube in the 2010s.

### Cultural context

Mostly women, often mothers, teachers and students; social, generous (sticker swaps, giveaways) and commercial at the
same time. It offers a creative, low-stakes hobby with a practical excuse. It also sits inside influencer marketing:
"hauls" (showing purchases) and affiliate links drive buying.

### Key quotes (short)

- Erin Condren "Our Story": began as "a stationery business operating out of her children's playroom."
- Happy Planner "About": "It all started at a baseball game in 1998."
- A planner user in *Money* (2019): planning made her "more emotionally stable."
- A psychiatrist in the same article: when the need to stick with the plan crowds out self-care, "that's a red flag."

## 4. Philosophy and principles

There's no manifesto, but repeated beliefs are:

- **Pretty makes it stick.** If opening the planner is pleasant, you'll open it.
- **Your planner, your rules.** Customisation is the point (disc-bound, custom covers, choose layouts).
- **Decorating is self-care.** A calm, creative ritual.
- **Community.** Sharing spreads, swapping stickers, supporting small shops.
- **Functional vs deco is a spectrum.** Many land on "functional with a little pretty".

**Problem it solves:** planners that feel like work; wanting a creative outlet with a use.

### Planner and layout
The EC LifePlanner comes in 7×9 and 8×10 in, with vertical, horizontal, hourly and dashboard layouts, 80 lb paper,
about $32–68 (EC LifePlanner page). The vertical layout's 1.5 in columns set the sticker standard. Happy Planner
pages are 4.5×7 (mini), 7×9.25 (classic) and 8.5×11 (big).

### Functional stickers
Plannerface's glossary: "stickers designed for a specific organisational purpose, like marking appointments or
reminders." Types: icons (small pictures: car, pill, payday), checklists, boxes (full 1.5×1.9 in; half 1.5×0.95 in;
quarter 1.5×0.475 in), headers, date covers, trackers, countdowns, page flags.

### Decorative stickers
Themed art, seasonal sets, characters, quotes and lettering. Sold as kits, sheets and books.

### Kits
A "full kit" (Plannerface: 6.5 sheets) themes a whole week; "mini kits" and "monthly kits" exist. Kits are sized for
one planner's boxes, which locks people into one planner.

### Washi tape
mt tape is made from washi (Japanese paper): tears by hand, repositions, can be written on (Nippon.com). Used for
borders, dividers, tabs and covering mistakes.

### Stamps
Reusable icons and boxes; cheaper per use than stickers, slower to use. Black ink stamps suit a monochrome planner.

### Plan with me and hauls
Videos of someone decorating a week, often with a kit from a sponsor, and videos showing new purchases. Social,
calming to watch for some; for others they drive spending.

## 6. Setting it up

A calm, functional-first setup:

1. **Pick a planner you already have** (or a Journalwright Studio book). Don't buy a new one to start.
2. **List your recurring things** (shift, meds refill, payday, bus pass, trash day, laundry, appointment).
3. **Pick one icon per thing.** Keep to 8–12. Same icon every time.
4. **Choose a medium:** pencil-drawn icons, one black stamp set, or one sheet of printed icon stickers.
5. **Decide where icons go** (left edge of a day, or a fixed spot). Never over headers or scan codes.
6. **Set a decorating budget** in minutes and money, if you decorate at all.

**First day:** add icons to today's fixed events only. **First week:** do one weekly session (10–15 minutes) to place
icons for the week. At the end, drop icons you never used.

## 7. Daily practice

1. **Open to today** (5 s).
2. **Glance at icons** (5 s): shift, meds, bus.
3. **Add any new icon** (15 s).
4. **Tick or cross off** as things happen.
5. **Optional deco** (0–5 min): a stamp or one sticker in the sketch box if it helps you settle.

Weekly session (the "plan with me" moment, 10–30 min): calendar first, icons second, deco last and optional.

## 8. Rhythms

- **Weekly:** plan and place icons; optional deco. Many people's main ritual.
- **Monthly:** monthly spread; bills and appointments icons; tidy the sticker stash.
- **Quarterly:** review what stickers you actually use; give away or swap the rest.
- **Yearly:** new planner "setup"; archive the old; budget check for the hobby.
- **Stash upkeep:** a "use first" folder prevents buying duplicates.

## 12. Community practice

- **What people do:** weekly "plan with me" sessions, monthly setups, sticker swaps, giveaways, subscription boxes,
  meetups and conventions. Reddit's r/plannerAddicts and r/Planners, Instagram and YouTube are hubs.
- **Customisations:** covers, tabs, dashboards, sticker storage binders, custom stamps, Cricut-cut stickers.
- **Aesthetic camp:** full kits, every box filled, colour themes per week, "pretty for the photo".
- **Functional camp:** icons and checklists only, often in one colour; "it's a tool".
- **Middle:** functional stickers plus one decorative element.
- **Minimalist backlash:** posts about sticker hoards, "no-buy" months, returning to pencil and one pen.
- **Health talk:** many users describe ADHD, anxiety or chronic illness and say decorating is calming; others say it
  became avoidance or overspending. Both are common; neither is studied.

## 14. Tools and supplies

- **Pens:** gel and fine liners; heavy markers bleed through thin paper.
- **Paper:** EC uses 80 lb; Journalwright Studio books print on KDP paper, so test markers on the last page.
- **Stickers:** matte removable paper accepts pen; glossy doesn't. Vinyl is durable but thick.
- **Washi:** 15 mm most common; can be removed from most papers.
- **Stamps:** clear acrylic block plus pigment ink; small black icon stamps suit B&W.
- **Punches:** disc punches (Happy Planner), hole punches for rings.
- **Printable sticker paper:** full-sheet matte labels or round labels for home printing.
- **Cutting machines:** Cricut and Silhouette "print then cut"; expensive.
- **Rulers and corner rounders.**

## 15. Digital and hybrid versions

- **Digital planners:** hyperlinked PDFs in GoodNotes or Notability, with digital sticker packs. Unlimited stickers
  and no ongoing cost per use, but screen time and a different kind of hoarding.
- **E-ink:** reMarkable, Boox, Kindle Scribe can't show colour stickers; functional icons in greyscale work. The X4 is
  1-bit, so icons need to be crisp black and white.
- **Scanning:** stickers and washi can cover text and confuse OCR; glossy stickers glare. Journalwright Studio pages must keep
  the header, frame, SEND TO strip and Data Matrix clear.
- **Lost vs paper:** the tactile ritual. **Gained:** undo, no cost per sticker, no stash.

## 16. Evidence and research

No trials study decorated planners. What exists:

| Study | Sample | Method | Finding | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tholander & Normark 2020, CHI | Bullet journalers | Qualitative | Crafting information supports reflection, self-creation, acceptance of imperfection, and resistance to "business-like efficiency" | Small, qualitative; BuJo, not sticker planners |
| Ayobi et al. 2018, CHI | Instagram BuJo photos and conversations | Qualitative content analysis | People adapt trackers to practical and emotional needs, use design as mindful reflection, and learn by sharing photos | Public, self-selected posts |
| FioRito, Geiger & Routledge 2020, *Art Therapy* | Group scrapbooking, 3 sessions over 6 weeks | Pre–post | More nostalgia proneness and group connectedness; people in low states planned to use scrapbooks for comfort | No control; scrapbooks, not planners |
| McMains & Kastner 2011, *J Neurosci* | fMRI | Lab | Multiple items in view compete for neural representation; grouping reduces competition | Basic science; supports "less clutter, clear grouping" design, not planners directly |

## 18. Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| Spending creep (kits, hauls, subscriptions) | Set a monthly cap; one functional sheet; no subscriptions |
| Decorating instead of doing | Plan first, decorate after, with a timer |
| Perfectionism ("ruined" spread) | Pencil first; accept imperfection; a sticker can cover a mistake |
| Sticker hoarding ("too pretty to use") | Use-first folder; give away quarterly |
| Visual overload | Monochrome; icons only; one deco element max |
| Locked into one planner size | Choose icon-sized stickers, not box kits |
| Comparison with influencers | Mute; follow functional accounts |
| Stickers covering needed info or scan codes | Fixed icon spots; keep scan zones clear |
| Bleed and ghosting from markers | Test on the last page; fine liners |

## 19. Accessibility and adaptations

- **ADHD:** icons give fast visual cues; stickers can be the dopamine that gets the planner opened. Risk: hyperfocus on
  decorating and impulse buying. Use a small fixed icon set and a timer.
- **Autism and sensory needs:** bright, busy spreads can overload; glossy stickers glare. Prefer matte, monochrome,
  one consistent icon style, and predictable placement. Some autistic people love collecting; a bounded collection
  (one binder) helps.
- **Chronic illness and low spoons:** a sticker is faster than writing on bad-hand days; pre-printed icon sheets save
  effort. Peeling tiny stickers can hurt; use larger ones or stamps.
- **Depression:** decorating can be a gentle activity on low days; the guilt of a blank, undecorated week can hurt.
  Plain pages are fine.
- **Anxiety:** a consistent visual system can reduce uncertainty; comparing to influencers can raise it.
- **Trauma:** no specific issues; privacy of a decorated planner shown online matters.
- **Dyslexia and dysgraphia:** icons over words help. Keep icons distinct in shape, not just colour.
- **Low vision:** high contrast, larger icons (0.5 in or more), no thin pastel lines.
- **Motor or hand pain:** stamps with large handles, bigger stickers, sticker lifters; washi is easier than tiny die-cuts.
- **Trans and gender-diverse people:** community is often welcoming; mainstream products lean heavily feminine or
  "mom". Neutral icons and your own labels avoid that. HRT and appointment icons are useful functionally.
- **Shift workers:** vertical layouts built for 9-to-5 "morning, day, night" don't fit; icons for shift type (day,
  night, off) are more useful than deco.
- **Low income:** the biggest barrier. Free printable icons, one black pen and pencil icons do the functional job;
  a library printer or a pack of plain labels costs little.
- **Colour blindness:** don't encode meaning in colour; use shape and icon.

## 23. Glossary

- **Functional sticker:** carries information (icon, box, checklist).
- **Decorative (deco) sticker:** decoration only.
- **Kit:** coordinated stickers for a week (full, mini, monthly).
- **Full box / half box / quarter box:** sticker sizes for EC vertical columns (1.5 in wide).
- **Header:** a strip at the top of a day or section.
- **Date cover:** a sticker covering the printed date.
- **Washi tape:** decorative paper tape.
- **Washi sticker paper:** sticker stock with a washi-like, slightly sheer finish.
- **Foiled:** metallic-foil stickers.
- **EC / ECLP:** Erin Condren LifePlanner.
- **HP:** Happy Planner.
- **Disc-bound:** pages on plastic discs, removable.
- **Vertical / horizontal layout:** columns vs rows per day.
- **Plan with me (PWM):** a filmed planning session.
- **Haul:** a video or post showing purchases.
- **Stash:** a person's sticker and supply collection.
- **Sticker swap:** trading stickers by mail.
- **Print then cut:** printing stickers and cutting them with a machine.
- **No-buy:** a period of not buying supplies.

## 24. FAQ

**Do I need stickers to plan well?** No. Functional icons can be drawn or stamped.

**Functional or decorative?** Functional if you want speed; one decorative element if it helps you open the planner.

**Will stickers stick to Journalwright Studio pages?** Yes on KDP paper; matte stickers accept pen. Keep them off the header,
frame, SEND TO strip and Data Matrix.

**How much should I spend?** Set a cap. A functional setup can be one printed sheet a month.

**Is decorating procrastination?** It can be. Plan first, decorate second, with a timer.

**Do stickers help ADHD?** Many users say yes; there's no study. A small consistent icon set is the part most likely to
help.

**Are colour stickers a problem for scanning?** They can cover content and glare. B&W matte is safest.

**Are washi and stickers archival?** Many aren't; adhesives can yellow or lose grip over years. Don't rely on them to
hold important notes.

**Can I use stickers on the X4?** No, it's a screen; icons there are drawn by the firmware.

### d. Proposed method layout: "Functional planner"

This layout is not among the built-in method layouts yet (today: original, Bullet Journal daily, Hobonichi, Five Minute, Theme System).

1. `sky`
2. `events`
3. `icons`: work, bus, pill, coin, text, sleep
4. `top`: n 3
5. `timeline`: from 6, to 22, every 2
6. `care`
7. `stickers`: circle, n 3
8. `divider`
9. `body`: dots

### e. Don't adopt

- **Colour-coding.** The books are B&W; colour-only meaning also fails colour-blind users.
- **Decorative kits, seasonal themes, character art.** Busy, costly, against the calm design.
- **Hauls, subscriptions, "new drop" cadence.** Consumerism; a calm book doesn't need a monthly drop of new accessories.
- **Rewards, streak stickers, badges** (on paper or device). Out of scope and pressuring.
- **Box sizes tied to one brand's columns.** Size for icons and circles, not EC boxes.
- **Stickers or washi in the scan zones.** They break the scan system.
